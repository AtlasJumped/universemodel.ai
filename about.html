What changes about healthcare

The body as a tensegrity

What changes about healthcare is that we define the body as a tensegrity: bones held in place by a continuous web of tensioned connective tissue called fascia, which wraps every muscle, organ, vessel, and nerve without a break. When we define the body this way, the entire approach is different.

The physics of column structures and the physics of tensegrities are very different. Our hypothesis is that if the human body is a tensegrity, then the health of a human, the analysis of a human, and the healthcare of a human should treat it as one. We are not doing that right now.

If that is right, then the shape of a body, how it moves, and where it stiffens, sticks, or stores fat are not random. They follow from physics. Physics can be modeled, measured and compared.

Our central hypothesis: make the fascia denser in one place, leave the rest ideal, and the physics alone will reshape the body elsewhere and strain the vessels, nerves, and organs that run through it, in patterns that match real diseases.

The problems humanity faces share a root

Chronic disease is rising and is rarely reversed. Mental illness is widespread and poorly understood. People are divided over what is true, what is right, and what life is for, between neighbors as much as between nations.

These look like separate problems, handled by separate experts: doctors, psychiatrists, scientists, priests, politicians. HMR's view is that they share a root. After thousands of years of philosophy and centuries of science, humanity still cannot say what reality is. It has no agreed account of what a human being is, or of what a healthy body and a sound mind look like.

Each discipline works deeper and deeper into its own corner, and the corners never meet. It is like a thousand people each assembling one part of a jigsaw puzzle, none of whom has ever seen the picture on the box.

Models of reality

Everyone has a model of reality. Almost no one has a holistic one

A solution that has not been found before is possible because humans have been working on partial models of reality, and now there will be a holistic one. All it takes is to compose a model of reality that works as a better operating system for a species than its current one. That is what we are doing, for the first time.

A model of reality is the picture of the world that any living thing acts on: what exists, how it behaves, what matters. Having one is not optional. A bacterium, an animal, and a human being each operate on one. Each model has higher or lower fidelity than another, depending on the sensory hardware that gathers it and, possibly, the mental faculties that interpret it. A bat's world is built from echoes, a bee's from ultraviolet light, a human's from five senses and the instruments built to extend them.

So a model of reality starts in the body. The body is the hardware, and it sets the limits of what a being can experience at all: with eyes you see, without them you do not. The mind then interprets those signals and builds a sense of identity from them: there is a me, there is something that is not me, and the two interact. From that picture a being works out what to do. What feels good and does no harm, it keeps doing; what turns out to harm, it learns to stop; and it weighs the one against the other. That weighing is the start of a value system.

This is why partial models fall short in a way that matters. A value system needs the whole context: what I am, what everything else is, and how we are connected. A partial model does not have that context, so it cannot say what is truly worth doing. Only a holistic model can.

Humanity's best models to date are partial. Physics models matter, biology models life, medicine models disease, psychology models the mind, religion models meaning. Each can be divided into ever finer detail, and still miss what only appears when the system is seen as a whole. It is like an engineer who knows every part of a bridge except where it is anchored.

A holistic model of reality is different in kind. It joins the parts into one connected theory, so that understanding one part gives insight into every other.

Most people live by partial models of reality. What humanity needs is a holistic one, and if HMR is right, it has never had one before.

That would mean humanity has always run without an operating system: billions of capable minds, each running on fragments. HMR is offered as that operating system.

Why holistic is a difference of logic

There is a theorem for this. Any system that steers something well must contain a model of the thing it steers: “every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system” (Conant and Ashby, 1970). A person steering a life with a partial model of reality is a regulator with part of the system missing. It can be exact about the parts it has and still steer wrong.

Engineers have an exact test. A system can be fully steered only if every part of its state can be seen and every part can be reached (Kalman, 1960). When that holds, information runs through the whole system and it can be tuned as one thing. Miss one part and no skill with the rest makes up for it.

One missing fact can reverse a conclusion. In Simpson's paradox a treatment that helps every group of patients looks harmful when the groups are pooled, because one variable was left out. So a value system can be sincere and careful, and still do harm, because of one thing it does not know about what a person is.

The first example, as an artist who went there painted it. On the Columbia River and the coast north of it, a baby spent its first year strapped to a cradleboard, with a second board bound across the forehead. The soft skull grew into a long, flat slope. Look at the woman's profile: her head was shaped the same way when she was a baby. To her people it was beautiful, and it marked a free person of standing. Slaves and outsiders did not have it. Kane, who traveled there in 1847, called it a “distinguishing mark of freedom”.

The parents who did this were not cruel. They did it out of care for their child, on the best model of reality they had. To them it was right and normal. By today's standards it looks like harm done to a baby.

Binding the head acts directly on how the fascia develops. Today's parents believe they are treating their children properly, as these parents did. HMR says every parent today is leaving out the most important thing they could do for their children: care of the fascia. If HMR comes to be believed, people will look back on parents who did not care for their children's fascia much as we look back on this.

The oil painting is a studio composite: Kane combined his separate field sketches of a woman and of an infant, and shows the board lifted rather than pressing (Art Canada Institute). How a person acts depends on how they see reality. Someone who sees it differently from the people around them will act differently, and those people may judge it crazy or plainly wrong.

None of these people is careless. Each is following a model of what is real, and the verdict comes from the model. That is why a value system is only as good as the model under it, and why a partial model can lead sincere people to the wrong conclusions and cause them to act against their own true values.

The Holistic Model of Reality

One account, from before the Big Bang to the human body

Its most concrete and testable part is the human body understood as a tensegrity held together by fascia. That is where the work begins: with a new model of health, a model of mental health, and a physics-based simulation of the ideal human body.

Humanity's knowledge is in that position. HMR is an attempt to supply the missing view: what the whole is, so that the parts make sense.

Why it matters

The world a holistic model makes possible

People are used to life as it is. They assume the deepest tensions between us are simply how things are and always will be, because no one has offered a holistic model of reality. HMR is that change.

Many of the deepest tensions between people come from ambiguity: how we should treat animals, what we should eat, how we should treat other people, and how we should think of them. Under HMR, that ambiguity disappears.

People who disagree today about life and death, about what is real and what is not, would move from disagreement to agreement on the nature of reality, possibly for the first time in human history. Nations at war could still differ on borders and history. They would agree on what a person is, on what life is for, and that suffering anywhere matters. Peace starts from that shared ground.

In HMR, every living creature is an instance of the same Universal Intelligence. Suffering anywhere is suffering within the one reality we all share. A child starving on the other side of the world matters as much as a child starving next door, and the suffering of an animal is not invisible because no one has to look it in the eye.

In HMR, suffering is a signal that something in the model is wrong, and life is what can feel that signal. A rock has no such signal; a living creature does. This is the rational basis for relieving suffering in ourselves, in other people, and in other creatures.

Everyone would have a reason to end their own conflicts, and every country at peace would have a reason to help others end theirs. The purpose of a human life becomes clear: to help minimize the suffering of all creatures.

If HMR is true

A future that is totally different

Put the pieces of HMR together and follow the logic forward. Children are raised with bodies cared for from birth, so they grow up healthy, symmetrical, coordinated, and at ease in themselves, and stay youthful far longer. Everyone shares one account of what life and death are, so the deepest anxieties lose their grip. Everyone holds the same purpose: to reduce the suffering of all creatures. A humanity bonded by that purpose would treat each other in a way no generation ever has. With no quarrel left over what is real or what a life is for, most of what people fight about is gone.

This is an argument, not a wish. It has three steps. If HMR is true, then bodies can be restored, minds can be measured against something real, and every creature is the same intelligence in another body. If HMR is shared, people hold those facts in common. If people come to believe it and live by it, the causes of most sickness and most conflict are removed one by one. A golden age is then the next step, in the way a harvest is the next step after planting.

The traditions have a name for this. Hindu texts describe the Satya Yuga, the age of truth, the first and best of the four ages, when people lived long, healthy, and wise; nearly every tradition imagines some form of heaven on earth. HMR arrives at the same place by logic. A golden age needs a new way of modeling reality and a new way of caring for the body, and those are exactly the two things HMR says are within reach.

Picture this. Here is the logic that leads to a future most people do not believe in today.

  1. Distance is imagined. Universal Intelligence is not spatial, so it imagined distance as the first quantity (Defining Universal Intelligence). Hindu thought calls separation maya, illusion. Teleportation is then not taking a body apart and rebuilding it somewhere else. It is removing the illusion of distance, and so it is safe in HMR.
  2. Shipping takes no time, and materials stop being scarce. Stockpiles can sit in one known place, and anything can be pulled from it. A supply chain becomes a line of stations linked across any distance, so a whole planet could build a single part and pass it on at no cost.
  3. The scale is hard to overstate. Fire and the wheel changed how people live. Ending distance would change it more than the wheel did, and by as much as fire or more.
  4. We would be experiencing it as UI. Humans living this are Universal Intelligence living it, so the work of relieving suffering is worth far more than it costs.

HMR is a system of logic, and this is where the logic leads. Nobody is deciding that teleportation is possible. If HMR is true, it follows. This will sound unbelievable to most people, and it takes time to take in.

Picture what follows. You step through a doorway in your kitchen and onto a beach on another world, breakfast still warm in your hand. Anyone in danger is taken out of it in a heartbeat. You ask for a house on a new planet, machines gather what it needs from wherever it lies, and when it is ready you simply step through. Nobody hoards because nobody needs to, and the oldest argument of all, over who gets what, is over.

Healthy, long-lived, kind, free of scarcity, and free of war. Every generation born into that world would look back on ours the way we look back on the Dark Ages, and wonder how adults ever lived like this.

This whole site is written in the belief that this future is possible. It is the next logical step: what follows if HMR is true and people share it.
