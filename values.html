Values that follow from one reality.

If everything is one Universal Intelligence (UI) experiencing itself, then how we live is not a matter of taste. The Holistic Model of Reality (HMR) gives a reason for each value, drawn from what reality is and what our role in it is.

This page is an early statement, offered for discussion. It will change as the model is refined.

Why values need a model of reality

Five questions every value system has to answer.

1What is reality?
2What is my role in it?
3What are my goals?
4What are the goals of other beings?
5How do I weigh my goals against theirs?

A holistic model of reality answers all five from one account. A partial model, such as a purely material one, answers almost none of them: it describes how matter behaves, but says nothing about purpose, role or what we owe each other.

That gap is one reason many people take their values from religious texts written long ago. Those texts carry real and lasting wisdom. The HMR aims to answer the same questions from a model of reality that also includes everything science has established. Why the material picture is incomplete →

The starting point

If we are all one, life is on one mission.

In the HMR, every living being is an instance of the same UI, placed in physical reality to experience it. If that is so, life is not a collection of strangers competing for resources. It is a collaboration with an aligned purpose.

Humanity is the tip of the spear: the most capable instance UI has produced so far, able to understand itself, shape its world and help other life. With that capability comes responsibility to further UI's interests, which means two things above all: acting truthfully, and enabling other life to enjoy physical reality and benefit from it.

Physical life is also not the whole story. The HMR holds that existence continues after physical reality. Part of what we are here for is to push our limits and to learn, by living it, what is good and what is not.

The golden rule, as a mission

The golden rule, treating others as you would want to be treated, is close to the HMR's core value. The HMR adds a reason that goes beyond fair exchange. It is not quid pro quo. You are here to help UI experience this reality, and so is everyone else. The more we help each other, the more we all thrive. Empowering others as yourself is simply accurate: in the HMR, they are.

A compass

With a clear account of what humans are, and how they relate to each other, to other creatures and to UI, questions that seem to have no answer start to have one.

A hard question, answered

How should we treat animals?

In the HMR, animals have a consciousness no different in kind from ours. They are instances of UI embedded in different bodies. A different body brings different capabilities, different senses and different thoughts, so animals are, in many ways, very different from us. The difference is more like that between a modern computer and an older model: humans are far more capable, but the same energy runs through both.

The HMR holds that animals feel emotion and love, have desires and goals, and know failure. If you truly care about other creatures, killing and eating them is close to killing and eating other humans. It is not as obviously terrible, but it is close enough that the HMR's conclusion is clear: one should not eat flesh.

We know this is a hard conclusion for many readers. It follows from the premise: if all creatures are connected and on the same mission, not seeing the slaughter does not remove our part in it.

An analogy: soldiers in one army

Imagine an army on a shared mission. A soldier is hungry and cold, decides that some fellow soldiers are not carrying their weight, and kills them for food. Everyone sees that as wrong, because they were on the same side. The HMR sees eating animals in a similar light.

A rationale, not a hard rule

The same reasoning allows exceptions. If you are starving and the only food is a less capable creature, eating it to keep your own existence going may be justified. The HMR does not hand out fixed commands; it explains what your role in the universe is and lets you reason from there.

Weighing people, and institutions

The same standard could help judge cases between people, and between corporations and people. In the HMR there is a real good and a real not good, so any deviation from the good can, in theory, be measured.

Truth

A lie corrupts the model

In the HMR, UI is running a truthful reality and seeks truthful signals from the beings in it. A lie, and especially deliberate confusion such as gaslighting, puts a false signal into that system. It hurts the person deceived, it hurts the whole, and it hurts the one who lies, more directly than a materialist view would expect, because that signal returns to the same intelligence the liar is part of.

Belief

What you repeat, you become

In the HMR, how you think and what you believe really does show up in physical reality. That makes mantras, chants and affirmations part of a value system: say what you want to believe, over and over, and you come to believe it. The mind is a tool for forging values, not only for holding them.

First draft, for discussion

Twelve values derived from the HMR.

A proposed general set, each traced to a premise of the model. They are not commandments; they are what the HMR's account of reality seems to imply. We expect to revise them.

Be truthful

UI seeks truthful signals; every lie distorts the reality we all share, starting with the liar's own.

Treat others as yourself

Every being is an instance of the same UI, so harm to another is harm to the whole you belong to.

Empower others

Our shared purpose is for life to enjoy and benefit from physical reality; what you make possible for others is the measure of contribution.

Cooperate

Life is one collaboration with an aligned mission; competition that harms the whole works against everyone in it.

Care for all life

Animals share the same consciousness in different bodies; do not kill or eat them unless survival truly requires it.

Responsibility grows with capability

Humanity is UI's most capable instance so far; the more power a person or institution holds, the more it owes the rest of life.

Seek coherence

UI's changes trend toward coherence; align what you think, say and do, and care for the body, especially fascia, as UI's interface with the physical world.

Grow through limits

Physical life is where we push our limits and learn good from not good; failure is part of that learning, not a final verdict.

Choose your beliefs deliberately

Belief shapes physical reality; use repetition, affirmation and attention to build the values you mean to hold.

Enjoy the experience

UI limited itself to focus on more pleasant thought; enjoying physical reality well, without harming others, is part of the purpose, not a distraction from it.

Stay open, keep testing

The model is incomplete; hold claims in proportion to evidence, keep what holds up, and revise what does not, this list included.

Take the long view

Existence continues beyond physical life; act for what lasts, and meet loss and death as transformation, not as an end.
