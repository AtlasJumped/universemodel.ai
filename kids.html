For parents

Do you have kids?

Then this page is for you. How you raise a child depends on what you believe a child is, and on what you believe is good for that child. The Holistic Model of Reality (HMR) describes a world in which what is good for a child is very different from what most people think today.

You love your children and you want the best for them. We take that as given. Our claim is that science and medicine have not handed you the instructions for what is best, and that parents everywhere have been steered away from a much better practice. We are not making a gentle suggestion. We are asking for a change in how people care for their children and for themselves.

What is at stake

Every day counts while a child is growing.

The human body is a tensegrity: bones held in a web of fascia under tension. A child's web is being built right now, and whatever shapes it during growth, it keeps. The HMR predicts that children whose web is cared for every day will grow up more athletic and more symmetrical than their peers, with clearer skin and easier movement.

Think about what children are teased for: being clumsy, being bad at sports, how they look. In the HMR's view, much of that is not fate or genes. It is a tensegrity that grew slightly out of shape and was never helped back. A body in its intended shape is coordinated, symmetrical and attractive, because that is what the design looks like when nothing is pulling it off line.

This is not a reason for dread. It is a reason to start. Each day of care is a day of growth pointed in the right direction.

We cannot convince you with one website. What we can do is give you the argument and the tools, and ask you to find out for yourself whether we are right. Because if we are, everyone should change what they are doing.

How to check us

These are the HMR's predictions, not established medicine; no trial has yet followed children raised this way. So test it where you can see the result: learn the protocol on your own body for a few weeks, read the evidence that the body is a tensegrity, and judge.

Two worlds, two childhoods.

Raised in a materialist world

  • The body is taken as it comes. If nothing hurts, it is called healthy.
  • Skin care stops at a bath and the occasional lotion. No one works the connective tissue underneath.
  • Narrow, pointed shoes from the first steps; long hours sitting from the first school day.
  • Exercise is sport and effort, loaded onto whatever body the child happens to have.
  • A child is told the universe is an accident, and is left to work out alone why so many adults are anxious, depressed or at war with each other.

Raised in an HMR world

  • The body is a tensegrity being built, and every day of growth shapes it. Health is measured against the ideal, not against "nothing hurts yet".
  • Daily fascia care is as ordinary as brushing teeth: warm water, layered moisturizer, massage, gentle scraping, long easy stretches.
  • Feet are free to spread, in foot-shaped shoes, from the start.
  • Strength training comes after the web is in good shape, not before.
  • A child is told what they are: an instance of the same Universal Intelligence as everyone else, with a part to play. Universal Intelligence →
What we understand best

No one is caring for children's fascia.

Very few parents anywhere put their children through a strict daily moisturizing routine, or work their tissue with scrapers, massage and long holds. Almost no one has been told it matters. Right now the world has no fascia health protocol at all.

Yet childhood is exactly when it matters most. Children have not yet built up the adhesions and dense patches that adults carry. Their fascia is growing, and it is shaped by whatever forces act on it while it grows: posture, shoes, sitting, sport, and care or the lack of it. In the HMR's view, puberty is the most important window of all, because the body grows fastest then. Why puberty matters →

Adults have carried the imperfections they grew into for life. With a protocol, an adult can work to undo them. A child can avoid most of them in the first place.

Start as soon as possible. Every month of growth without care is a month the web is shaped by chance. In the HMR's view, learning this protocol and helping children grow into the tensegrities they were meant to be is every parent's responsibility.

Family healthcare

This is hard to test in clinics, and it does not need a clinic. Parents learn the protocol on themselves, then teach it to their children, and children learn to care for themselves. The HMR's view of puberty

Puberty is the tensegrity reaching its limits.

Medicine describes puberty through hormones, and the hormones are real. The HMR adds a mechanical picture on top. As a child nears full height, the skeleton has grown about as far as the fascia web allows, and the growing frame starts to push against the web. Where the web is even, it stretches evenly. Where it is tight or stuck, the push lands in the wrong places and stretches them out of shape.

In the HMR's hypothesis, the familiar troubles of those years are symptoms of a tensegrity being pushed out of shape: the skin breaking out, the clumsiness of a body that suddenly does not fit itself, the cracking voice.

And puberty ends, in this view, not because it has been completed well, but because the web has adapted to its imperfections. It lays down adhesions to stabilize itself, and it can stabilize in a poor position. That position is the adult body most people then live in for life.

This is a hypothesis of the HMR, not a finding. Medicine attributes acne and the changing voice to hormones acting on skin glands and the growing larynx; the HMR does not dispute those facts, and proposes that the state of the web decides how hard those years land.

Why it matters to a parent

If this is right, the years before and during puberty are the best chance a person ever gets. A web that enters puberty supple and even has room for the frame to grow into. A web that enters it tight gets locked that way.

Body and mind

A child's fascia and a child's mind are one system.

Adults today carry a heavy load of anxiety and depression, and medicine struggles to explain it or to agree on what it is seeing (mental health today). The HMR's claim is that much of this begins in the body. A web under constant uneven tension sends the brain a constant signal that something is wrong, and a mind living with that signal for decades has a harder time being calm.

So caring for a child's fascia is also caring for the adult mind that child will have. Add an honest account of what they are and how to treat others, and you have given them the three things the HMR says mental health rests on: a body at ease, beliefs that are true, and a community that understands them. Stated plainly

The link between body tension and mood has research behind it; the claim that fascia care in childhood prevents adult mental illness is the HMR's prediction and has not been tested. We think it is one of the most important things anyone could test.

What it looks like at each age.

BabiesTouch and moisture

Gentle daily massage and moisturizer, head to toe. In a large trial of daily emollient from birth, the only problem found was slightly more skin infections in the first year, so use clean hands and simple products (Chalmers et al., Lancet, 2020). Watch for allergies.

Young childrenFree feet, slow stretching

Barefoot time and foot-shaped shoes. Play-based long stretches. Make the evening moisturizer and massage a ritual they look forward to.

Older childrenLearning the tools

Gentle scraping, foam rolling, vibration, breathing and humming. Teach them to close their eyes and feel the place being worked.

PubertyThe window

The most important years. Daily care, long holds and toe spacing while the body is growing fastest, and strength training only once the web is in good shape.

Imagine a generation

Every child, raised the same way.

Picture every child in the world growing up with a body cared for from birth and a clear account of what they are. Bodies that move freely and do not hurt. Minds that are not puzzled by the basic questions, because they have been answered. Children who know that everyone else is an instance of the same intelligence they are, and so have every reason to treat each other well.

Those children would be far less confused about why so many of the adults around them are anxious and depressed. And they would be much less likely to become those adults.
