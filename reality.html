A new model of reality

Where today's picture of reality runs out.

Modern science is the most successful description of physical reality humanity has ever had. It is also incomplete, more deeply than most people realize. It cannot yet say what most of the universe is made of, how gravity fits with everything else, what life is, or why anyone is conscious at all.

What we use now

Two great theories that do not fit together.

Newton's laws described motion and gravity so well that they guided science for two centuries. Einstein then showed they were an approximation: in general relativity, gravity is the curvature of space and time. At the smallest scales, quantum mechanics describes matter and light with extraordinary precision.

Each theory is confirmed in its own domain. But they rest on different pictures of what reality is, and no one has found a way to combine them into one theory. Where they must meet, they disagree badly: the energy of empty space predicted by quantum theory exceeds what cosmology observes by a factor of roughly 10120, which has been called the worst prediction in physics (Weinberg, 1989).

And the visible universe is a small part of the whole. By the best current measurements, ordinary matter makes up only about 5% of the universe's contents; the rest is attributed to dark matter and dark energy, whose nature is unknown (Planck Collaboration, 2020).

In short

Physics describes the rules of physical reality with great precision. It does not yet have one account of those rules, and it does not say why they exist, what most of the universe is made of, or how experience arises from matter.

The biggest gap

No one knows what gravity is.

Gravity is the most familiar force there is. It holds you in your chair and the planets in their orbits. And after more than three hundred years, it is still the force physics understands least.

  • It will not join the rest of physics. The other three forces have quantum descriptions. Gravity does not, and every attempt to give it one, from string theory to loop quantum gravity, remains unconfirmed.
  • It breaks down where it matters most. At the center of a black hole and at the Big Bang, general relativity predicts infinities: places where the theory stops describing anything.
  • It is absurdly weak. Between two protons, the electric force is about 1036 times stronger than gravity. No one knows why the difference is so enormous.
  • It does not add up. Galaxies spin and cluster as if they held far more mass than we can see. Either most of the matter in the universe is invisible, or our account of gravity is wrong at large scales. Both possibilities are still open.

“Gravity must be caused by an agent acting constantly according to certain laws; but whether this agent be material or immaterial is a question I have left to the consideration of my readers.”

Isaac Newton, letter to Richard Bentley, 25 February 1692/3. In the same letter he called action at a distance through empty space “so great an absurdity” that no competent thinker could accept it. The Newton Project transcription.

Newton gave us the law of gravity and deliberately left open what gravity is. Three centuries later, it is still open.

What a material view has not explained

Open questions at the foundations.

None of these proves that materialism is wrong. Each is a place where a purely material account of reality has no agreed answer, and where some of the founders of modern physics themselves expected one to be needed.

Entanglement

Connected across any distance

Measure one of two entangled particles and the other's result is correlated with it instantly, however far apart they are. Einstein argued this meant quantum mechanics was incomplete (Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen, 1935). Bell showed it could be tested (Bell, 1964), and experiments confirmed it (Aspect et al., 1982), including three loophole-free tests in 2015 (Hensen et al.; Giustina et al.; Shalm et al.). The work won the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics. Reality is not "local" in the everyday sense.

Measurement

What makes one outcome real?

Quantum mechanics describes a system as a spread of possibilities until it is measured, and then one outcome appears. A century on, physicists still disagree about what a measurement is and why it has that effect; the main interpretations offer incompatible pictures of reality (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Measurement in Quantum Theory").

Consciousness

Why is there experience at all?

Neuroscience can map which brain activity accompanies which experience. It cannot yet explain why any physical process is accompanied by experience in the first place: the "hard problem" of consciousness (Chalmers, 1995). A material account explains what the brain does, not why there is someone there.

Gravity

The force that will not fit

No quantum theory of gravity has been confirmed, and general relativity breaks down at black holes and at the Big Bang. More above.

The dark universe

95% of everything, unidentified

Dark matter and dark energy are names for effects, not explanations. Decades of searches have not detected a dark-matter particle, and the nature of dark energy is unknown (Planck Collaboration, 2020).

The beginning

Why there is something, and why these laws

Cosmology traces the universe back to the Big Bang, but not what came before it, why it began in such an improbably ordered state, or why its constants fall within the narrow range that allows atoms, stars and life. These questions have no agreed scientific answer.

What the gaps add up to

Science is incomplete, and seriously so.

Put the list together and the picture is plain. Science cannot say what most of the universe is made of. It cannot reconcile its two best theories. It cannot say what gravity is, what came before the Big Bang, why the laws are what they are, why mathematics describes them, what life is, how it began, or why there is experience at all.

These are not small gaps at the edge of knowledge. They sit at the foundations, under everything else we think we know. A materialist account of reality is not a finished picture with a few details missing. It is a precise description of how physical reality behaves, with no agreed account of what that reality is.

That is why a holistic model of reality is needed today: one that keeps everything science has established, and also offers an account of the foundations that science leaves open.

The foundations, in one table
  • What is matter? About 5% identified.
  • What is gravity? Unknown at the quantum level.
  • Where did it all come from? Unknown.
  • Why these laws? Unknown.
  • Why does math work? Unknown.
  • What is life? No agreed definition.
  • What is consciousness? Unexplained.
  • What is a healthy human? Never defined.
For readers who trust science

If you value reason, these gaps are a question for you.

Many people who think of themselves as scientific, rational and guided by values hold a materialist view of reality without ever having examined its foundations.

Being scientific means believing in proportion to the evidence. Being rational means noticing when a view no longer makes sense. Being values-driven means caring what our beliefs do to other people. Taken seriously, those three commitments lead to two honest questions:

Do I keep believing in something that, at its foundations, does not make sense?

What harm do I cause if I stay comfortable in it?

The harm is not abstract. A view of reality decides what we think a person is, what we think is possible for a body, what we treat and what we dismiss, what we teach children, and what we owe each other.

You cannot build values on a reality you do not know

Every value system rests on some picture of what reality is: what a person is, whether life has a purpose, what happens after death, whether anything beyond matter matters. If science cannot say what reality is, then a value system that rests on science alone is resting on an open question.

When science is this incomplete, it is a stretch for anyone to assume they know what follows from it.

What we have seen

In conversation after conversation, people lean on science as if it settled what reality is. But when they look closely at the gaps, few of them actually believe it does. And the less someone believes the materialist picture is complete, the more room, and the more reason, they have to want a holistic model of reality.

The Holistic Model of Reality

One account, from before the Big Bang to today.

The HMR proposes that physical reality emerges from Universal Intelligence (UI), a non-physical intelligence whose changes over time trend toward greater coherence. The laws of physics are the stable rules that make a complex physical reality possible. Seen this way, the open questions above stop being anomalies. Non-local connection, the role of observation and the existence of experience are what one would expect if intelligence comes first and matter second.

We believe the HMR is the most holistic model of reality yet put forward. It offers one narrative that runs from before the Big Bang to the present, accounts for science and for human consciousness, contradicts no established science, and finds a place for what the world's religions have described. Breadth is not proof, and the HMR is not yet empirically established. That is why we are testing its most tangible part first: a new model of health.
