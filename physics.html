The HMR physics paradigm.

The HMR keeps every equation physics has confirmed. What it changes is the framework the equations are read in: space, time and matter are not the bottom layer of reality, Universal Intelligence (UI) is. Read that way, physics' open problems change shape. This page sets them side by side, then makes a serious attempt at the hardest one, gravity.

Each row is a question physics has not settled. In the right column, how the HMR reframes it. These are the HMR's readings: frameworks to think and test with, not established results.

The hardest problem

Curved spacetime, from the first distance.

On the Science Bridge, the mathematics runs from the first distance to flat spacetime, the spacetime of special relativity. The next step is curved spacetime: gravity. This is the HMR's route, written out step by step. It does not invent new physics. It walks a road physicists already know, the thermodynamics of spacetime (Jacobson, 1995), with the pieces of causal set theory, and shows where the HMR's first distance fits into it.

The result is a complete chain to Einstein's equations, with every link labeled. Some links are established physics. Some follow from the HMR. Some are assumptions the HMR has to add, and three are still open. One prediction has been tested, and it did not pass. None of this proves Universal Intelligence; mathematics alone cannot do that. What it does is take the idea as far as careful mathematics can go today, and mark exactly where trained physicists have to take over.

1. The first distances are scattered, not a grid HMR-derived

Each act of distinction is an element; each relation says which came before which. Space-time volume is the count of elements: N elements fill a volume V = N ℓ₀⁴. Could the elements sit on a regular grid? The HMR's own counting rules that out. On a cubic grid, a surface with direction n is crossed by

(|nx| + |ny| + |nz|) / ℓ₀²  relations per unit area,

which varies by up to a factor of √3 with direction, so the strength of gravity would depend on which way you faced. It does not. If the relations carry no structure beyond themselves HMR assumption, they are scattered at random, and a random scattering picks out no direction and no moving frame (Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin, 2009) standard. That gives relativity's single light cone, with c = ℓ₀ / t₀, and it predicts no change in the speed of light with energy, which matches gamma-ray burst observations (Vasileiou et al., 2013).

2. What a boundary counts, and the size of the first distance HMR assumption calculated

A boundary of area A is crossed by γ relations per ℓ₀², each carrying information s. Its entropy is

S = η A,   η = s γ / ℓ₀²

γ can be calculated. Count the pairs of elements that straddle the boundary with nothing in between, the "horizon molecules" of causal set theory. For randomly scattered elements in four dimensions the answer is exact (Barton et al., 2019), and we reproduced it by direct numerical integration (0.17320):

γ = √3 / 10 ≈ 0.173

If each crossing relation is one binary distinction, "me" or "not me", then s = ln 2. Black holes fix η = 1 / (4 ℓP2) (Bekenstein, 1973), so

ℓ₀ = 2 √(s γ) · ℓP  ≈ 0.69 ℓP ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻³⁵ m

This settles the old question of the factor of 1/4: it is not a separate mystery but the statement of how large the first distance is, a little under one Planck length. Honest limits: this trades G for ℓ₀ rather than predicting G, and other reasonable ways of counting "crossing" give other values of γ (counting every element whose only links lead across gives roughly 2, and ℓ₀ ≈ 2.3 ℓP).

3. The temperature standard

An accelerating observer finds empty space warm, at the Unruh temperature

T = ħ κ / 2π

This follows exactly from relativity's symmetry plus quantum theory (Bisognano and Wichmann, 1975; Unruh, 1976). The HMR does not derive quantum theory; it borrows ħ, positive energy and locality HMR assumption.

4. Coherence displaces distance, as an equation HMR assumption

This is the HMR's central physical claim, now given a number. When energy crosses a boundary, the entropy it carries is recorded as new crossing relations:

s · ΔN = δQ / T  ⟹  ΔN = −(2π / ħ s) ∫ λ Tab ka kb dλ dA

For s = ln 2, that is about 9 new relations for every ħ of energy times distance crossing the boundary. This is the step where gravity enters.

5. Curvature is the acceleration of the relation count standard

Follow a bundle of light rays and let n be the number of relations crossing it. Raychaudhuri's equation (1955) says how the bundle's cross-section changes, and in HMR terms that reads:

Rab ka kb = − d² ln n / dλ²

Curvature, the thing Einstein's equations describe, is how fast the count of first distances speeds up or slows down along a ray of light.

6. Einstein's equations HMR-derived

Steps 4 and 5 must agree for every light ray through every point. That forces

Gab + Λ gab = (8π G / c⁴) Tab,   with  G = c³ ℓ₀² / (4 ħ s γ)

once matter's energy is conserved (the Bianchi identity does the rest) standard. Two things come out that the HMR said in words before. Gravity is set by how many first distances a surface holds. And any energy spread evenly through empty space drops out of the equations, so "empty space carries no weight" is now a result, not a separate claim. Λ, the cosmological constant, appears as a free constant, which opens the door to step 8.

7. What is still open open

  • Smooth spacetime from discrete relations. No rule for adding relations, in the HMR or in any discrete approach to gravity, has been shown to produce a smooth, four-dimensional spacetime. Most random orderings look nothing like space. This is one of the central unsolved problems of quantum gravity.
  • Which count is the entropy. Step 2 calculates γ for one definition of "crossing". Why that count, and not another, should be the entropy is not settled; one numerical study of entanglement, in a simplified one-space-plus-one-time model, found a volume law rather than an area law until near-zero modes are removed.
  • Quantum theory itself. The temperature in step 3 relies on it, and the HMR does not yet derive it.

8. A test, and its result: dark energy prediction not passed

If space-time is a count of N elements, that count fluctuates by about √N, and Λ inherits the fluctuation. Rafael Sorkin made this argument around 1987, roughly a decade before dark energy was discovered, and it gives the observed size (Ahmed, Dodelson, Greene and Sorkin, 2004):

Λ ~ 1 / √N  ≈ 4 × 10⁻¹²² in Planck units;  measured: 2.85 × 10⁻¹²²

The prediction is that dark energy wanders randomly over cosmic time. How strongly it wanders is set by one number, α = ½ (ℓP / ℓ₀)², and simulated universes survive only if α is about 0.02 or less (Das, Nasiri and Yazdi, 2023). Step 2 now fixes that number, so the test can be run:

α = 1 / (8 s γ)  ≈ 1.0   (s = ln 2, γ = √3/10);   needed: α ≲ 0.02

We report the result straight. With a binary first distinction, the HMR's version of this prediction fails by a factor of about fifty: dark energy would swing far too violently. The more generous count in step 2 (γ ≈ 2) still misses by a factor of four or more. To pass, each crossing relation would have to carry about 35 to 100 bits rather than one, and nothing in the HMR says why it would. Separately, a recent analysis finds that typical histories of the wandering model fit supernova and microwave-background data poorly (Das, Nasiri and Yazdi, 2024).

So this prediction does not stand in its present form. Steps 1 to 6 are unaffected: they reproduce Einstein's gravity exactly, which also means that for now the HMR's gravity predicts nothing different from general relativity.

Where this leaves us

This page now has a chain of equations from the first distance to Einstein's gravity: the old question of the 1/4 answered, the grid ruled out, the number of crossing relations calculated, the HMR's claim about empty space turned into a result, and one prediction taken all the way to a test, which it did not pass. That is what honest progress looks like. The route reproduces known gravity; it does not yet go beyond it, and it is not a proof.

What remains is not a list of small next steps. It is the same wall every theory of quantum gravity stands in front of: showing that discrete pieces must add up to smooth space-time, and finding where quantum theory comes from. The HMR goes about as far with the idea of Universal Intelligence as mathematics can go before it needs physicists working on it full time. Proving the source of reality mathematically will take many people. This page is an invitation to them: every assumption is named, so every one can be attacked, tested or improved.
