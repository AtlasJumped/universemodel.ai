HMR physics paradigm.

HMR keeps every equation physics has confirmed. What it changes is the framework the equations are read in: space, time and matter are not the bottom layer of reality, Universal Intelligence (UI) is. Read that way, physics' open problems change shape. This page sets them side by side, then makes a serious attempt at the hardest one, gravity.

The mechanics, in physical units

What one thought is worth.

The Math page builds a mathematics whose only object is a thought. This section asks what that gives physics: a unit, a way to write any object as numbers of that unit, an exact form of one of HMR's ideas about gravity, and a plain list of what is missing.

The short version: the unit and the counts are exact arithmetic once three assumptions are granted. The rule for how thoughts follow one another gives time and does not yet give space. No particle, and none of the constants of nature, is derived. HMR's physics, as it stands, reproduces what is known and predicts nothing new.

The unit

Three things are assumed, and each is marked where it enters the gravity route below.

  1. The order of thoughts is spacetime-like in the large, so that volume is the number of thoughts: V = N ℓ₀⁴. This defines ℓ₀. Whether a grown order is spacetime-like is open
  2. A surface of area A is crossed by γ A/ℓ₀² relations of the kind counted there, with γ = √3/10. established for random scatters
  3. Each crossing relation carries one two-way distinction, s = ln 2 of entropy. an assumption of HMR What would confirm it: deriving s from the rule of growth.

Every object as two numbers of distance

HMR proposes that distance is the fundamental quantity, and that every object could be reduced to numbers of distance that carry all of its energy. For the total energy of an object at rest, this is literally true, in two ways at once.

nC = Compton length / ℓ₀
nS = gravitational radius / ℓ₀
nC × nS = 1 / (2 s γ) = 4.165

The first number falls as energy rises and the second rises, so their product is the same for every object.

For a hydrogen atom, nC = 1.877 × 10¹⁹ and nS = 2.219 × 10⁻¹⁹. The energy that goes into those numbers already includes mass, motion, binding and the energy tied to spin.

established arithmetic. It is an identity, not a finding: the product of the two lengths is twice the Planck area for any mass in any system of units. HMR reading for the proposal, and it is matched for total energy only. Spin and charge are pure numbers, not distances, and neither n is a whole number.

Matter adds distance inside itself. A ball of matter has more distance from its surface to its centre than its surface area predicts, by G M / (3 c²): 1.5 millimetres for the Earth, about 490 metres for the Sun. This is standard general relativity, and it is the closest thing in physics to HMR's “matter displaces distance”. established as displacement: a reading

The whole and its parts

HMR's idea is that two things together are more than the two apart, with a trade-off. In counts, that is exact. When two black holes with horizons of radius r₁ and r₂ merge, the number of relations crossing the horizon gains a cross term, up to

8 π γ r₁ r₂ / ℓ₀²

which is the m₁ × m₂ of Newton's law in another form, while the total mass cannot rise and usually falls, radiated away. The count goes up; the weight goes down. established as the trade-off: a reading

A count is not a weight

A flat, empty region of N thoughts has about N²/20 related pairs and weighs nothing. So “only relations carry weight” needs care: only structure in the relations carries weight; an even background of relations does not. In the gravity route this is the fact that evenly spread energy drops out of the equations. established

One idea that does not hold

That the smallest things, a neutrino or a degree of heat, are a couple of units across. It is the other way round: the less energy, the longer the pattern. A neutrino of 0.05 electronvolts corresponds to about 3.5 × 10²⁹ units, far longer than an atom.

The rule of growth, tested as physics

What it gets right

Thoughts in an order, with no meaning to the birth order of unrelated ones, is the structure physicists call a causal set. Counting thoughts gives volume, and the longest chain gives time. The classical rule for growing one is exact and checked.

established

What it does not give

Space. For every choice of its free numbers the classical rule grows something long in time with no spatial structure in the large. In our runs, a true four-dimensional scatter measures 3.96 to 3.99 dimensions at every size; grown patterns wander between about 1.4 and 4.2 and settle on none.

established theorem; our runs are single runs

The early burst

After an origin, the width of the pattern rises in a burst and then stays constant. The volume of a thought's past grows at a steady rate of about 0.3 per step for five to seven e-folds, then linearly. A de Sitter curve fits, and so do curves with no meaning. This is not inflation: the rate is some 40,000 times above what observation allows.

numerical a handful of runs, not a theorem

So the classical rule is a template for UI's law, not the law. What has to change, and what the change would open up, is on the Math page. Until a rule exists that grows three dimensions of space, everything in the gravity route below that says “if the order is spacetime-like” stays an if.

What would change the verdict

The shortest honest list. Each item says what would have to be shown. None has been.

  1. A rule of growth that yields space

    Shown by a dimension near 4 holding steady over at least a hundredfold range of sizes, by theorem or by simulation at 100,000 thoughts or more. For rules within ordinary probability this is a published open problem, and those who posed it expect the answer to need quantum theory. Everything else waits on this.

  2. Inflation from a closed moment, in that rule

    Shown by growth at a steady rate below 8 × 10⁻⁶ per step, for 60 e-folds or more, followed by slower growth, with ripples of the measured tilt (0.965) and size (2.1 × 10⁻⁹).

  3. One pure number

    A lasting pattern in the grown order with a computable period, and the ratio of two such periods equal to a measured mass ratio: 206.768 for the muon and electron, or 1836.153 for the proton and electron. HMR derives no particle and none of the roughly thirty pure numbers of physics today.

  4. The bit, derived

    A reason from the rule of growth why a crossing relation carries exactly one bit, and which count is the entropy. Then 4.34 thoughts per Planck volume becomes a prediction that can be tested.

  5. A quantum rule for births

    One that reduces to the classical template when interference is switched off and gives linked outcomes up to the quantum limit and no further. This is where “what was one never stops being one” would become mechanics, and where ħ would either be derived or shown to be an input.

  6. A number for coherence

    One quantity that rises when matter clumps and also ranks a living thing above a hot gas, on test cases fixed in advance. Today the word is used for both, and no single number does both jobs.

The hardest problem

Curved spacetime, from the first distance.

On the Science Bridge, the mathematics runs from the first distance to flat spacetime, the spacetime of special relativity. The next step is curved spacetime: gravity. This is HMR's route, written out step by step. It does not invent new physics. It walks a road physicists already know, the thermodynamics of spacetime (Jacobson, 1995), with the pieces of causal set theory, and shows where HMR's first distance fits into it.

The result is a complete chain to Einstein's equations, with every link labeled. Some links are established physics. Some follow from HMR. Some are assumptions HMR has to add, and three are still open. None of this proves Universal Intelligence; mathematics alone cannot do that. What it does is take the idea as far as careful mathematics can go today, and mark exactly where trained physicists have to take over.

1. The first distances are scattered, not a grid HMR-derived

Each act of distinction is an element; each relation says which came before which. Space-time volume is the count of elements: N elements fill a volume V = N ℓ₀⁴. Could the elements sit on a regular grid? HMR's own counting rules that out. On a cubic grid, a surface with direction n is crossed by

(|nx| + |ny| + |nz|) / ℓ₀²  relations per unit area,

which varies by up to a factor of √3 with direction, so the strength of gravity would depend on which way you faced. It does not. If the relations carry no structure beyond themselves HMR assumption, they are scattered at random, and a random scattering picks out no direction and no moving frame (Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin, 2009) standard. That gives relativity's single light cone, with c = ℓ₀ / t₀, and it predicts no change in the speed of light with energy, which matches gamma-ray burst observations (Vasileiou et al., 2013).

2. What a boundary counts, and the size of the first distance HMR assumption calculated

A boundary of area A is crossed by γ relations per ℓ₀², each carrying information s. Its entropy is

S = η A,   η = s γ / ℓ₀²

γ can be calculated. Count the pairs of elements that straddle the boundary with nothing in between, the "horizon molecules" of causal set theory. For randomly scattered elements in four dimensions the answer is exact (Barton et al., 2019), and we reproduced it by direct numerical integration (0.17320):

γ = √3 / 10 ≈ 0.173

If each crossing relation is one binary distinction, "me" or "not me", then s = ln 2. Black holes fix η = 1 / (4 ℓP2) (Bekenstein, 1973), so

ℓ₀ = 2 √(s γ) · ℓP  ≈ 0.69 ℓP ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻³⁵ m

This settles the old question of the factor of 1/4: it is not a separate mystery but the statement of how large the first distance is, a little under one Planck length. Honest limits: this trades G for ℓ₀ rather than predicting G, and other reasonable ways of counting "crossing" give other values of γ (counting every element whose only links lead across gives roughly 2, and ℓ₀ ≈ 2.3 ℓP).

3. The temperature standard

An accelerating observer finds empty space warm, at the Unruh temperature

T = ħ κ / 2π

This follows exactly from relativity's symmetry plus quantum theory (Bisognano and Wichmann, 1975; Unruh, 1976). HMR does not derive quantum theory; it borrows ħ, positive energy and locality HMR assumption.

4. Coherence displaces distance, as an equation HMR assumption

This is HMR's central physical claim, now given a number. When energy crosses a boundary, the entropy it carries is recorded as new crossing relations:

s · ΔN = δQ / T  ⟹  ΔN = −(2π / ħ s) ∫ λ Tab ka kb dλ dA

For s = ln 2, that is about 9 new relations for every ħ of energy times distance crossing the boundary. This is the step where gravity enters.

5. Curvature is the acceleration of the relation count standard

Follow a bundle of light rays and let n be the number of relations crossing it. Raychaudhuri's equation (1955) says how the bundle's cross-section changes, and in HMR terms that reads:

Rab ka kb = − d² ln n / dλ²

Curvature, the thing Einstein's equations describe, is how fast the count of first distances speeds up or slows down along a ray of light.

6. Einstein's equations HMR-derived

Steps 4 and 5 must agree for every light ray through every point. That forces

Gab + Λ gab = (8π G / c⁴) Tab,   with  G = c³ ℓ₀² / (4 ħ s γ)

once matter's energy is conserved (the Bianchi identity does the rest) standard. Two things come out that HMR said in words before. Gravity is set by how many first distances a surface holds. And any energy spread evenly through empty space drops out of the equations, so "empty space carries no weight" is now a result, not a separate claim. Λ, the cosmological constant, appears as a free constant, which opens the door to step 8.

7. What is still open open

  • Smooth spacetime from discrete relations. No rule for adding relations, in HMR or in any discrete approach to gravity, has been shown to produce a smooth, four-dimensional spacetime. Most random orderings look nothing like space. This is one of the central unsolved problems of quantum gravity.
  • Which count is the entropy. Step 2 calculates γ for one definition of "crossing". Why that count, and not another, should be the entropy is not settled; one numerical study of entanglement, in a simplified one-space-plus-one-time model, found a volume law rather than an area law until near-zero modes are removed.
  • Quantum theory itself. The temperature in step 3 relies on it, and HMR does not yet derive it.

8. A borrowed prediction, tested: dark energy borrowed not adopted

This step is not part of HMR. It is another theory's prediction, causal set theory's, that we tried on to see whether it fit, because it would have given HMR a test of its own.

If space-time is a count of N elements, that count fluctuates by about √N, and Λ inherits the fluctuation. Rafael Sorkin made this argument around 1987, roughly a decade before dark energy was discovered, and it gives the observed size (Ahmed, Dodelson, Greene and Sorkin, 2004):

Λ ~ 1 / √N  ≈ 4 × 10⁻¹²² in Planck units;  measured: 2.85 × 10⁻¹²²

The prediction is that dark energy wanders randomly over cosmic time. How strongly it wanders is set by one number, α = ½ (ℓP / ℓ₀)², and simulated universes survive only if α is about 0.02 or less (Das, Nasiri and Yazdi, 2023). Step 2 now fixes that number, so the test can be run:

α = 1 / (8 s γ)  ≈ 1.0   (s = ln 2, γ = √3/10);   needed: α ≲ 0.02

We report the result straight. Combined with HMR's binary first distinction, this borrowed prediction fails by a factor of about fifty: dark energy would swing far too violently. The more generous count in step 2 (γ ≈ 2) still misses by a factor of four or more. To pass, each crossing relation would have to carry about 35 to 100 bits rather than one, and nothing in HMR says why it would. Separately, a recent analysis finds that typical histories of the wandering model fit supernova and microwave-background data poorly (Das, Nasiri and Yazdi, 2024).

So HMR does not adopt it. Nothing in HMR itself is contradicted: steps 1 to 6 are unaffected and reproduce Einstein's gravity exactly. What it means is that HMR's gravity does not yet make a prediction that differs from general relativity, and finding one remains open.

Where this leaves us

This page now has a chain of equations from the first distance to Einstein's gravity: the old question of the 1/4 answered, the grid ruled out, the number of crossing relations calculated, HMR's claim about empty space turned into a result, and a prediction borrowed from another theory tried and set aside because it did not fit. That is what honest progress looks like. The route reproduces known gravity; it does not yet go beyond it, and it is not a proof.

What remains is not a list of small next steps. It is the same wall every theory of quantum gravity stands in front of: showing that discrete pieces must add up to smooth space-time, and finding where quantum theory comes from. HMR goes about as far with the idea of Universal Intelligence as mathematics can go before it needs physicists working on it full time. Proving the source of reality mathematically will take many people. This page is an invitation to them: every assumption is named, so every one can be attacked, tested or improved. Free fall is the path of most thoughts

Between two events, a freely falling body takes the path on which the most time passes. In a pattern of thoughts shaped like space and time, the time along a path is the number of thoughts in the chain, so the free path is the longest chain. A thrown ball rises because clocks run faster higher up, and comes back because moving fast costs time. Its arc is the best trade between the two.

established as Einstein's rule; the longest chain measures time in flat space (Brightwell and Gregory, 1991) “most thoughts”: HMR reading

Why match Einstein at all, if his theory predicts its own failure?

Because it fails in two places only. Einstein's equations have passed every test made of them, from falling apples to merging black holes, so any replacement must give the same answers wherever they have been tested. At the centre of every black hole and at the start of the universe they give infinities, which is a theory announcing that it has stopped working.

HMR is measured against the first and is free at the second. What it has there today is one thing: the count is finite, so there is no infinity. It does not yet have the formula for what replaces the centre. established the formula: open
