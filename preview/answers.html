The universe

What came before the Big Bang, and why is there something rather than nothing?

Cosmology, philosophy

The problem
The equations of cosmology run back to a hot, dense beginning 13.8 billion years ago and then stop working. They do not say what came first or why anything exists.
Who has worked on it
Georges Lemaître, Stephen Hawking, Roger Penrose, Alan Guth
Why science struggles
Physics takes space, time and matter as the bottom layer, so it has nothing left to explain them with.
Why HMR can answer
HMR starts one level deeper, with Universal Intelligence (UI), which is not in space or time.
HMR's answer

What came before was Universal Intelligence: awareness with unlimited potential and no order yet. Its first act was a distinction, me and not me, and that distinction was the first distance. As for why there is something rather than nothing: in HMR there never was nothing. Mind came before matter, and UI did not begin. Why UI itself exists, HMR does not explain; that is where it starts.

What was the Big Bang?

Cosmology

The problem
The universe expanded from a hot, dense state, and a burst of exponential growth called inflation fits the data, but the cause of both is unknown.
Who has worked on it
Georges Lemaître, Alan Guth, Andrei Linde
Why science struggles
The theories fail before the first 10⁻⁴³ seconds, and the field said to drive inflation has never been observed.
Why HMR can answer
HMR describes what UI was doing at that moment.
HMR's answer

The Big Bang was the moment UI's first complete model of itself closed. UI had been building up relations, and when the model was complete, the completion was a catalytic event: a thought exploding into a greater thought. The burst of growth that followed is what physics calls inflation.

What is gravity?

Physics

The problem
Newton called it a force; Einstein showed it is the bending of space and time. Neither says why mass bends space, and gravity cannot be combined with quantum theory.
Who has worked on it
Isaac Newton, Albert Einstein, Ted Jacobson, Erik Verlinde
Why science struggles
Every attempt to treat gravity like the other forces produces infinities.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR, space is counted in units of distance, and gravity is about that count.
HMR's answer

Gravity is reality's trend toward coherence. Matter is coherent structure, and coherent structure displaces distance, so bodies are drawn together. Written out, the number of first distances crossing a surface plays the role of entropy, and Einstein's equations follow. Parts of that route are still open.

What is dark energy?

Cosmology

The problem
The expansion of the universe is speeding up. About 68% of the universe's energy is assigned to whatever causes it, and nobody knows what that is.
Who has worked on it
Saul Perlmutter, Brian Schmidt, Adam Riess
Why science struggles
It is a placeholder: a number in an equation with no known source.
Why HMR can answer
HMR does not need a substance to do the pushing.
HMR's answer

Dark energy is not a substance. It is the continuing work of the universe modeling itself: new relations, and with them new distance, which we see as space expanding faster. Whether one says space grows or its contents shrink is a choice of units, since the observations are the same; counted in first distances, it is space that grows. HMR has no formula for the amount yet.

What is dark matter?

Astronomy, particle physics

The problem
Galaxies rotate and cluster as if they held about five times more mass than can be seen.
Who has worked on it
Fritz Zwicky, Vera Rubin
Why science struggles
Decades of detectors have found no dark matter particle.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR, not everything that gravitates has to be matter.
HMR's answer

Dark matter is coherence that does not shine: structure in the distribution of distance itself, felt as gravity without being matter that gives off light.

Why is the energy of empty space so small?

Physics

The problem
Quantum theory says empty space holds energy. The natural calculation of how much is about 10¹²⁰ times larger than what is measured: the worst prediction in physics.
Who has worked on it
Steven Weinberg, Yakov Zeldovich
Why science struggles
Quantum theory counts the jitter of every field at every point, and nothing in physics says why that should not weigh anything.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR only structure in the relations of UI's model carries weight. An even background of relations does not.
HMR's answer

Because almost nothing in empty space counts. Only structure carries weight, and the uncounted jitter of “nothing” is not structure. In HMR's route to gravity this comes out of the mathematics: energy spread evenly through empty space drops out of the equations.

What happens at the center of a black hole?

Physics

The problem
General relativity predicts points of infinite density, where the theory itself breaks down. Black holes also seem to destroy information, which quantum theory forbids.
Who has worked on it
Roger Penrose, Stephen Hawking
Why science struggles
An infinity means the equation has stopped describing the world, and no accepted theory replaces it there.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR, distance has a smallest unit.
HMR's answer

Matter falling in is packed tighter and tighter until its parts are one first distance apart. Nothing can be squeezed below that, because nothing was ever divided below it. So the center is not a point of infinite density. It is a core of the densest structure there can be, small but finite. What that core is like, and what it does next, HMR cannot yet say. The infinities belong to the idealized mathematics, not to reality.

What happens when a quantum system is measured?

Physics

The problem
A quantum system holds many possibilities at once until it is measured, when one becomes real. What counts as a measurement has been disputed for a century.
Who has worked on it
Niels Bohr, Erwin Schrödinger, John von Neumann, Eugene Wigner
Why science struggles
Physics has no place in its equations for the observer.
Why HMR can answer
HMR puts consciousness at the bottom, not at the end.
HMR's answer

One of the system's possibilities becomes definite. In HMR a measurement is UI making that one possibility definite in its own experience. Consciousness is not a late arrival in the universe that has to be fitted in afterward; it is the ground of the process. HMR has no mathematics for this step yet.

How can two particles stay connected across any distance?

Physics

The problem
Entangled particles stay correlated in ways no signal could carry. Einstein called it “spooky action at a distance”. Experiments confirm it.
Who has worked on it
Albert Einstein, John Bell, Alain Aspect
Why science struggles
If space is fundamental, the connection has nothing to travel through.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR, distance was made; it is not fundamental.
HMR's answer

Because they were never two separate things. Things that were one in UI never stopped being one. Their correlation ignores the distance between them because distance was added afterward.

Why is there a speed limit, the speed of light?

Physics

The problem
Nothing travels faster than light. Relativity takes this as a starting assumption and does not explain it.
Who has worked on it
Albert Einstein, Hendrik Lorentz
Why science struggles
A postulate is where explanation stops.
Why HMR can answer
HMR says what a step of time and a unit of distance are.
HMR's answer

Because a change cannot spread faster than one unit of distance per step of UI's thought. Time is the sequence of UI's thought, and that ratio, one first distance per step, is the speed of light.

What is time, and why does it only run forward?

Physics, philosophy

The problem
The laws of physics work equally well backward, yet we remember the past and not the future. Quantum theory and relativity also use two different ideas of time.
Who has worked on it
Ludwig Boltzmann, Arthur Eddington, Julian Barbour
Why science struggles
Physics can describe time's direction (entropy rises) but traces it to an unexplained starting condition.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR time is something UI does.
HMR's answer

Time is the sequence of UI's thought. It runs one way because maturation does: each thought is built on the ones before it. Order grows locally while total entropy rises, and the two fit together.

Why are the laws of nature just right for life?

Physics, cosmology

The problem
Many constants sit in the narrow range that allows atoms, stars and life. Small changes would give a dead universe.
Who has worked on it
Martin Rees, Brandon Carter, Fred Hoyle
Why science struggles
The options on offer are luck, or an unobservable multitude of other universes.
Why HMR can answer
HMR treats the laws as chosen.
HMR's answer

Because they were chosen. The constants are the rules UI holds fixed so that many beings can share one stable world. They are not a lucky draw.

Why does mathematics describe the universe so well?

Mathematics, physics

The problem
Equations invented on paper turn out to govern nature. Eugene Wigner called this “the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics”.
Who has worked on it
Eugene Wigner, Galileo Galilei, Max Tegmark
Why science struggles
If matter came first, there is no reason it should obey thought.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR, thought came first.
HMR's answer

Because the universe is made of it. Mathematics is UI's ordered thought, built from a first unit: one comes from the first distance, two from the first difference.

Mind and life

What is consciousness?

Neuroscience, philosophy

The problem
Brain science can map which activity goes with which experience. It cannot say why any physical process is felt from the inside: the “hard problem”.
Who has worked on it
David Chalmers, Christof Koch, Giulio Tononi, Roger Penrose
Why science struggles
Physics describes behavior from the outside, and experience is not a behavior.
Why HMR can answer
HMR does not try to get mind out of matter.
HMR's answer

Consciousness is the ground of reality, not a product of matter. Physics describes the stable rules inside an experience; it was never going to find experience inside its rules. Each living being is an instance of UI experiencing physical reality.

What is life, and how did it begin?

Biology, chemistry

The problem
Biology has working definitions, such as NASA's “self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution”, but no agreed account of what life is or how the first cell arose.
Who has worked on it
Erwin Schrödinger, Stanley Miller, Stuart Kauffman
Why science struggles
Each field defines life for its own purposes, and the definitions do not connect to mind or origin.
Why HMR can answer
A holistic definition has to connect physics, mind and origin at once.
HMR's answer

Life is UI experiencing physical reality from the inside. It is the kind of matter UI does not steer piece by piece: created once, set free as an agent, and self-sustaining. As for how it began: in HMR's story it follows a second catalytic event after the Big Bang, an inversion, in which the universe, having defined its outside, turned to look within.

What is a human being?

Biology, philosophy, religion

The problem
Science describes the human animal; traditions describe a soul. Neither account has persuaded everyone, and medicine has no agreed definition of a healthy human.
Who has worked on it
Charles Darwin, René Descartes
Why science struggles
A partial model can describe a body or a mind, not what a person is for.
Why HMR can answer
HMR answers from what UI is doing.
HMR's answer

A human being is, in effect, UI's artificial intelligence: a body built to upgrade itself, able to understand its own source. Humans building AI now is a small-scale copy of what UI did when it made us.

Is there a God, and why do religions disagree?

Religion, philosophy

The problem
Most of humanity believes in a higher intelligence, under many names and with conflicting descriptions. Science has been unable to confirm or rule it out.
Who has worked on it
Every tradition's founders; among scientists, Max Planck, Johannes Kepler, Erwin Schrödinger
Why science struggles
God is not an object inside the universe that an instrument could detect.
Why HMR can answer
HMR treats the source as the starting point of the mathematics.
HMR's answer

Yes, in this sense: there is one source, Universal Intelligence. Religions disagree because each names and describes a different aspect of it. An intelligence able to generate physical reality could be understood as one, as many, or as everything. HMR does not prove UI; it takes the idea as far as mathematics can and invites testing.

What happens when we die?

Religion, medicine, philosophy

The problem
Every tradition has an account of death. Science describes the body's shutdown and stops there.
Who has worked on it
Every tradition; in research, Ian Stevenson, Sam Parnia
Why science struggles
If mind is produced by the brain, the question has no meaning; if it is not, science has no tools for it.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR, a life is an instance of UI, so the question is open in a way materialism cannot allow.
HMR's answer

HMR does not claim to know. It has hypotheses, stated as hypotheses: something carries on, and what carries on may differ for every life, depending on how fully it became what it was for; death may be overcome; a perfect tensegrity might not age; and the laws themselves may change as UI matures.

What is the purpose of life?

Philosophy, religion

The problem
Science deliberately says nothing about purpose. Traditions give answers that conflict.
Who has worked on it
Aristotle, Viktor Frankl
Why science struggles
A purely material model describes how matter behaves and has no room for what it is for.
Why HMR can answer
A holistic model includes what life is, so it can say what life is for.
HMR's answer

To enjoy and benefit from physical reality, to reduce the suffering of all creatures, and to take part in UI's maturation. Every living being is an instance of the same UI, placed in physical reality to experience it, so life is a collaboration with a shared purpose.

How should we treat each other?

Ethics

Value systems differ between cultures and people, and arguments about them rarely end.
Who has worked on it
Immanuel Kant, John Stuart Mill, the authors of the golden rule in many traditions
Why science struggles
Values need a full picture of what a person is and how people are connected. Partial models do not have it.

As you would want to be treated. The golden rule follows directly: the other person is, at root, what you are. Values built on what reality is can be shared from person to person and country to country, because they do not rest on custom.

What is health?

Medicine

The problem
Medicine defines health mostly as the absence of diagnosed disease. It learns what health is by studying people who are unwell.
Who has worked on it
WHO (1948 definition), Georges Canguilhem
Why science struggles
Without a defined healthy body there is nothing to measure against.
Why HMR can answer
Physics can define the ideal structure.
HMR's answer

Health is closeness to the perfect human body: an optimized tensegrity, bones floating in an evenly tensioned web of fascia. Every condition is a measurable distance from it.

Why do chronic pain and chronic disease keep rising?

Medicine

The problem
Chronic conditions are increasing and are rarely reversed. Many have no agreed cause.
Who has worked on it
Researchers across medicine; in fascia science, Carla Stecco, Robert Schleip, Helene Langevin
Why science struggles
Medicine studies organs and molecules one at a time; it is not trained to see the body as one tension structure.
Why HMR can answer
A tensegrity passes every local fault through the whole structure.
HMR's answer

HMR's hypothesis: because their common cause goes untreated. Imperfections in the fascia web (density, adhesion, lost glide, twist) change tension everywhere and press on what the web contains, and they sit upstream of many conditions. They build up through life, and medicine, with no model of the ideal body to measure against, treats what they produce downstream. The hypothesis is being tested by simulation.

What is mental illness?

Psychiatry, psychology

The problem
Two clinicians examining the same patient often disagree on the diagnosis, the categories overlap, and treatment is largely trial and error.
Who has worked on it
Emil Kraepelin, Thomas Insel, Joanna Moncrieff
Why science struggles
Psychiatry has no agreed account of what is real, or of a healthy body, to measure a mind against.
Why HMR can answer
HMR supplies both baselines.
HMR's answer

Mental illness is what follows when one or more of the three things mental health rests on is missing: a body at ease, beliefs that are true, and a community that understands you. HMR's model maps a person's state by how they relate to reality, to their body and to other people, in place of overlapping labels.

Why does exercise help some people and injure others?

Sports science, medicine

The problem
Exercise is recommended to everyone, yet injuries are common and results vary widely.
Who has worked on it
Sports medicine as a field
Why science struggles
Programs load the body without first asking what shape its structure is in.
Why HMR can answer
A tensegrity under load amplifies its own flaws.
HMR's answer

Because exercise strengthens whatever it is built on. On an imperfect tensegrity it strengthens the imperfections, and that is where the injuries come from. Optimize the tensegrity first, then build exercise on top. For a body already close to ideal, hard training is excellent.

Why is puberty so hard on the body?

Medicine

The problem
Acne, clumsiness and voice changes are attributed to hormones acting on skin, growth and the larynx.
Who has worked on it
Endocrinology as a field
Why science struggles
The hormonal account is real but says little about why some bodies come through evenly and others do not.
Why HMR can answer
HMR adds the mechanics of a growing tensegrity.
HMR's answer

Because the skeleton is growing against the fascia web. As it nears full height it pushes on the web, and where the web is tight, the push lands unevenly. Puberty ends when the web has adapted to its imperfections, sometimes locking in a poor position.

What are qi, meridians and chakras?

Traditional medicine, anatomy

The problem
Eastern traditions map channels of energy through the body. Western anatomy never found separate vessels for them.
Who has worked on it
Helene Langevin, Thomas Myers, Mae-Wan Ho
Why science struggles
Science looked for a new structure rather than a known one seen differently.
Why HMR can answer
HMR reads both through fascia.
HMR's answer

They are the fascia web, mapped from the inside by people who felt it. Meridians follow the body's long fascial lines and connective tissue planes, chakras mark where its horizontal sheets cross those lines along the midline, and qi is what the classics called the activity moving through them. Practices such as the bandhas tension and release the web. The claim that Eastern practice works on fascia is lightly tested, by supporters and critics alike.
