The method

How HMR answers a question

Every answer on this page is taken through the same sequence, in the same order, against the same models. The sequence is short enough to follow yourself. Try it on a question of your own.

  1. Say the question plainly

    Put it in one sentence, and say what kind it is: about the world, the body, the mind, or what to do.

  2. Start from HMR's core

    There is one Universal Intelligence. Physical reality emerges from it. Every person is an instance of it, living through a body.

  3. Take the question through the models, deepest first

    The story of Universal Intelligence, then mathematics and physics, then the body (a tensegrity held by fascia), then the mind (reality, self, others), then the values.

  4. Cross-check

    An answer must agree with every model and with everything science has measured. If two models pull apart, the deeper one governs, and the gap is written down as work to do.

  5. For "should I" questions, apply the values

    Ask what serves the wellbeing of every instance of Universal Intelligence involved: you, your body, the other people, and the truth. For a decision about treatment or medication, part of the answer is to talk to a clinician you trust.

  6. Say how firmly the answer stands

    It is established by measurement, or it follows by logic from HMR, or it is HMR's view, still being worked out. Each answer below carries its label.

  7. If the models give no answer that satisfies, say so

    That marks where HMR needs more research or more written down, and it goes on the list.

What is gravity?

Physics

The problem
Newton called it a force; Einstein showed it is the bending of space and time. Neither says why mass bends space, and gravity cannot be combined with quantum theory.
Who has worked on it
Isaac Newton, Albert Einstein, Ted Jacobson, Erik Verlinde
Why science struggles
Every attempt to treat gravity like the other forces produces infinities.
Why HMR can answer
In HMR, space and time are counts of thoughts and their relations, and gravity is about those counts.
HMR's answer

Things fall because a free body takes the path on which the most time passes, and time runs slower near matter. That part is Einstein's, and HMR keeps it. In HMR time is the count of thoughts along a chain, so free fall is the path of most thoughts. Why matter slows time is where HMR's own route begins. Matter is coherent structure that takes up distance, and gravity is set by how many first distances a surface holds. Written out step by step, this leads to Einstein's equations, with every assumption named and three steps still open. One is showing that separate thoughts add up to smooth space and time. HMR weighs other readings beside it: an inward flow of space, and a draw back to source. In the mathematics of thought itself attraction is already exact: the more that rests on a thought, the more likely the next thought is to build on it.

What is dark energy?

Cosmology

The problem
The expansion of the universe is speeding up. About 68% of the universe's energy is assigned to whatever causes it, and nobody knows what that is.
Who has worked on it
Saul Perlmutter, Brian Schmidt, Adam Riess
Why science struggles
It is a placeholder: a number in an equation with no known source.
Why HMR can answer
HMR does not need a substance to do the pushing.
HMR's answer

Dark energy is not a substance. It is the continuing work of the universe modeling itself: new relations, and with them new distance, which we see as space expanding faster. Whether one says space grows or its contents shrink is a choice of units, since the observations are the same. Counted in first distances, it is space that grows. The amount has a formula. If space and time are a count of N thoughts, the count wanders by about its square root, and the cosmological constant with it: Λ ≈ 1/√N. With one thought per Planck volume in our past that gives 4 × 10⁻¹²² against a measured 2.8 × 10⁻¹²². The argument is Rafael Sorkin's, made about a decade before dark energy was found. The number in front depends on the exact size of the first distance, which is in progress.

Why is the calculated energy of empty space so wrong?

Physics

The problem

Because the calculation gives weight to every possible tremble of empty space. That removes the enormous number, and it is now a result of the route, not a separate claim. The small amount that is measured has a formula. What the core is like and what it does next, including what becomes of the information that falls in, no model answers yet. That is on the task force's list.

What is mental illness?

Psychiatry, psychology

The problem
Two clinicians examining the same patient often disagree on the diagnosis, the categories overlap, and treatment is largely trial and error.
Who has worked on it
Emil Kraepelin, Thomas Insel, Joanna Moncrieff
Why science struggles
Psychiatry has no agreed account of what is real, or of a healthy body, to measure a mind against.
Why HMR can answer
HMR supplies both baselines.
HMR's answer

Mental illness is strain on a mind: how far it has moved from a sound one. HMR describes a sound mind in fourteen measures across three layers: whether a person's picture of reality is true, whether their picture of themselves is true and they are at ease in it, body included, and how they deal with other people. Each is scored from 0 to 3, so a label becomes a description, such as danger read too high with a braced body. Treatment aims at the measure: a wrong picture of reality is answered with a truer one, a strained body with the fascia protocol, missing values with values, isolation with people. This is a way of describing minds, not a reason to stop any treatment. If you are struggling or thinking about changing a medication, talk to a clinician you trust.

Why does exercise help some people and injure others?

Sports science, medicine

The problem
Exercise is recommended to everyone, yet injuries are common and results vary widely.
Who has worked on it
Sports medicine as a field
Why science struggles
Programs load the body without first asking what shape its structure is in.
Why HMR can answer
A tensegrity under load amplifies its own flaws.
HMR's answer

Because exercise strengthens whatever it is built on. An imperfect tensegrity carries force unevenly: lines that are dense or stuck take far more than their share and the lines around them go slack. Training it harder makes the imperfections stronger, and that is where injuries come from, why gains fade when training stops, and why the body redistributes mass in ways people do not want. It follows by logic from the model that the order matters: optimize the tensegrity first, then build exercise on top. For a body with an excellent tensegrity, hard training can be excellent.

Why is puberty so hard on the body?

Medicine

The problem
Acne, clumsiness, and voice changes are attributed to hormones acting on skin, growth, and the larynx.
Who has worked on it
Endocrinology as a field
Why science struggles
The hormonal account is real but says little about why some bodies come through evenly and others do not.
Why HMR can answer
HMR adds the mechanics of a growing tensegrity.
HMR's answer

Medicine describes puberty through hormones, and the hormones are real. HMR adds the mechanics. As a child nears full height the skeleton has grown about as far as the fascia web allows, and the growing frame pushes on the web. Where the web is even, it stretches evenly. Where it is tight or stuck, the push lands unevenly. Puberty ends when the web has adapted to its imperfections, by laying down adhesions, and it can settle in a poor position that most people then live in for life.
