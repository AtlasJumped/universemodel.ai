The Holistic Model of Reality

Humans have always lived by models of reality: myths, religions, philosophies, sciences. Each explains part of the world well. None has explained all of it in one theory that holds together from start to finish. The Holistic Model of Reality (HMR) is that theory.

A partial model

Explains one part of reality and leaves the rest as given. Materialism looks complete on the surface, because it claims everything is matter; but it cannot say where matter came from, why its laws exist, or why anyone is conscious. A model that leaves its own foundations unexplained is partial, however large it is.

A holistic model

Explains everything it covers from the same starting point, so nothing is left as a loose end: the origin of the universe, the laws of physics, life, the mind, health and how to live. When a whole society shares one, its people can agree on what is true, and everyone benefits from that.

The core of it

Intelligence first, fascia first

HMR asks for two changes at the foundations of how we think about reality. Everything else on this site follows from them.

  1. Physical reality emerges from Universal Intelligence (UI). Space, time and matter are not the bottom layer of reality. They are what a non-physical intelligence built when it gave its thoughts a stable form. Defining UI →
  2. Fascia is the body's primary system for health. The connective web that holds every muscle, organ, vessel and nerve in place sets the shape and tension of the whole body, and most of what goes wrong in a body can be traced through it. A new model of health →
Why a new framework

Physics has two superb theories that conflict at their foundations, and it cannot say what 95% of the universe is, what gravity is, or why there is experience at all. Either its math is wrong or its framework is. HMR keeps the math and changes the framework. Science today →

Shared now, tested hard

We think the theory is already good enough to read, understand and live by, so we are sharing it now. At the same time we are testing it more thoroughly than most theories ever are, starting with its most tangible part: a physics simulation of the ideal human body. Road Ahead →

The Holistic Model of Reality

One theory, from before the Big Bang to today

HMR proposes that physical reality emerges from UI, a non-physical intelligence whose changes over time trend toward greater coherence. The laws of physics are the stable rules that make a complex physical reality possible. Living bodies are the vessels through which that intelligence experiences what it has made, and each human is one such instance. Defining Universal Intelligence →

Seen this way, the open questions science leaves stop being anomalies. Non-local connection, the role of observation and the existence of experience are what one would expect if intelligence comes first and matter second.

We believe HMR is the most holistic model of reality yet put forward. It offers one narrative that runs from before the Big Bang to the present, accounts for science and for human consciousness, contradicts no established science, and finds a place for what the world's religions have described. Breadth is not proof. That is why, while we share it, we are also testing its most tangible part first: a new model of health.

How HMR earns a hearing

If a model of the human body derived from HMR can simulate, explain and help reverse real disease in ways that modern medicine cannot, then HMR, the theory it comes from, deserves a serious look.

In their words

Great scientists who saw more to reality than matter alone

You may have studied their science, but not what they believed about mind and matter. Each, in their own way, saw more to reality than matter alone.

“Geometry, which before the origin of things was coeternal with the divine mind and is God himself, supplied God with patterns for the creation of the world.”

Johannes Kepler, astronomer. The Harmony of the World (1619), Book IV, trans. Aiton, Duncan and Field.

“There is no matter as such. All matter originates and exists only by virtue of a force... We must assume behind this force the existence of a conscious and intelligent mind. This mind is the matrix of all matter.”

Max Planck. Lecture "Das Wesen der Materie", Florence, 1944 (Archive of the Max Planck Society), translated from the German.

“It from bit symbolizes the idea that every item of the physical world has at bottom, at a very deep bottom, in most instances, an immaterial source and explanation.”

John Archibald Wheeler, physicist. "Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for Links", 1989. Source

“Will it not turn out, with the further development of science, that the study of the universe and the study of consciousness will be inseparably linked, and that ultimate progress in the one will be impossible without progress in the other?”

Andrei Linde, cosmologist, a founder of inflationary theory. "Inflation, Quantum Cosmology and the Anthropic Principle", 2002. Source

“I regard consciousness as fundamental. I regard matter as derivative from consciousness.”

Max Planck, physicist, founder of quantum theory. Interview in The Observer, 25 January 1931. Source

“Science without religion is lame, religion without science is blind.”

Albert Einstein. "Science and Religion", written for the Conference on Science, Philosophy and Religion, New York, 1940. Source

“I believe in Spinoza's God, who reveals himself in the lawful harmony of all that exists, but not in a God who concerns himself with the fate and the doings of mankind.”

Albert Einstein. Telegram to Rabbi Herbert Goldstein, April 1929, translated from the German. Source

“This most beautiful system of the sun, planets, and comets, could only proceed from the counsel and dominion of an intelligent and powerful Being.”

Isaac Newton, physicist and mathematician. Principia, General Scholium (added 1713), trans. Andrew Motte, 1729. Source

“Whatsoever is, is in God, and without God nothing can be, or be conceived.”

Baruch Spinoza, philosopher. Ethics (1677), Part I, Proposition 15, trans. R. H. M. Elwes. Source

“I am very astonished that the scientific picture of the real world around me is deficient. It gives a lot of factual information... but it is ghastly silent about all and sundry that is really near to our heart... It knows nothing of beautiful and ugly, good or bad, God and eternity.”

Erwin Schrödinger, physicist, a founder of quantum mechanics. Nature and the Greeks (1954). Source

“Consciousness is a singular of which the plural is unknown.”

Erwin Schrödinger. What Is Life? (1944), Epilogue.

“The personal self equals the omnipresent, all-comprehending eternal self.”

Erwin Schrödinger, summing up the teaching of the Upanishads, Atman = Brahman. What Is Life? (1944), Epilogue.

“To put the conclusion crudely, the stuff of the world is mind-stuff.”

Arthur Eddington, astrophysicist. The Nature of the Physical World (1928). Source

“The universe begins to look more like a great thought than like a great machine. Mind no longer appears to be an accidental intruder into the realm of matter.”

James Jeans, physicist and astronomer. The Mysterious Universe (1930). Source

“It was not possible to formulate the laws of quantum mechanics in a fully consistent way without reference to the consciousness.”

Eugene Wigner, physicist, Nobel laureate. "Remarks on the Mind-Body Question" (1961). Source

“What we observe is not nature in itself but nature exposed to our method of questioning.”

Werner Heisenberg, physicist, Nobel laureate. Physics and Philosophy (1958). Source

“The more I examine the universe and study the details of its architecture, the more evidence I find that the universe in some sense must have known that we were coming.”

Freeman Dyson, physicist and mathematician. Disturbing the Universe (1979), p. 250.

“If the world is rationally organised and has meaning, then it must be the case.”

Kurt Gödel, mathematician and logician, answering his mother's question whether they would meet again after death. Letter of 23 July 1961. Source

“I don't need to believe. I know.”

Carl Gustav Jung, psychiatrist, on being asked whether he believed in God. BBC interview, Face to Face, 22 October 1959. Source

“The God of the Bible is also the God of the genome. He can be worshipped in the cathedral or in the laboratory.”

Francis Collins, geneticist, director of the Human Genome Project. The Language of God (2006), p. 211.

Einstein called entanglement "spooky action at a distance" and argued that quantum mechanics could not be a complete description of reality.

Letter to Max Born, 1947; Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen, Physical Review, 1935.
