The people and work that shaped HMR

HMR did not come from nowhere. These are the teachers, thinkers, scientists, and channels we learned the most from on the way here, with links to the best places to find their work.

None of them is affiliated with HMR, and none has endorsed it. Where we say what HMR shares with someone, that is our reading of their work.

Teachers and thinkers

Five people who were trying to do what HMR is trying to do

S

Sadhguru

Jagadish "Jaggi" Vasudev, born 3 September 1957 in Mysore, India. Living.

A yogi and teacher who founded the Isha Foundation in 1992, based in Coimbatore in southern India. The foundation teaches yoga and runs large public projects on rivers, trees, and soil.

Work and highlights

  • Isha Yoga and the Inner Engineering course. His book Inner Engineering: A Yogi's Guide to Joy was a New York Times bestseller, as were Karma and Death.
  • Designed the 112-foot Adiyogi statue at the Isha Yoga Center, inaugurated in 2017.
  • Rally for Rivers (2017), Cauvery Calling (2019), and the Save Soil journey of 2022: 100 days and about 30,000 km by motorcycle from London to southern India.
  • Awarded the Padma Vibhushan, one of India's highest civilian honors, in 2017.

What he is doing now

Save Soil continues as an international campaign. In 2026 he led a Mahasatsang at Batu Caves in Malaysia, attended by more than 8,000 people and described as the first held outside India. In India, the foundation's outreach arm now operates crematoria for the state of Bihar.

He and the foundation have also drawn criticism, including for some health claims, and the foundation is party to legal disputes, which it contests.

What HMR shares with him

We took the Inner Engineering course. In HMR's view his techniques are very good for fascia, and his values, though he does not know of HMR, are naturally in tune with it.

AW

Alan Watts

Born 6 January 1915 in Chislehurst, England. Died 16 November 1973 in Marin County, California.

A British-born writer and speaker who interpreted Buddhist, Taoist, and Hindu thought for Western audiences. He called himself a "philosophical entertainer".

Life and work

  • Moved to the United States in 1938. Served as an Episcopal priest and left the priesthood in 1950.
  • Taught at the American Academy of Asian Studies in San Francisco, and built a following through a weekly radio program on KPFA in Berkeley from 1953 to 1962.
  • Books include The Way of Zen (1957), Psychotherapy East and West (1961), The Book: On the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are (1966), and Tao: The Watercourse Way (1975).

His central idea

The feeling of being a separate self, an "ego in a bag of skin", is a mistake. Each of us is an aspect of one whole. He spent his life showing Western listeners how Eastern thought arrives there.

What HMR shares with him

He married East and West, which is what HMR sets out to do. His gift was saying it clearly. Listening to him opened us to ideas from the East that we had never come across growing up in the West, and it can reshape how a person thinks.

Many hours of his talks are on YouTube. We like to listen while falling asleep.

TM

Terence McKenna

Born 16 November 1946 in Paonia, Colorado. Died 3 April 2000 in San Rafael, California, of brain cancer.

An American ethnobotanist, author, and lecturer who argued for the responsible use of naturally occurring psychedelics, and one of the most original speakers of his time.

Life and work

  • Traveled to the Colombian Amazon with his brother Dennis in 1970, the journey told in True Hallucinations (1993).
  • Co-founded Botanical Dimensions, an ethnobotanical preserve in Hawaii, in 1985.
  • Books include The Invisible Landscape (1975, with Dennis McKenna), The Archaic Revival (1991), and Food of the Gods (1992).
  • Some of his best-known proposals, such as the "stoned ape" idea and novelty theory, are not accepted by science.

Dennis McKenna

Through Terence we came to his brother Dennis (born 1950), an ethnopharmacologist with a doctorate from the University of British Columbia, a founding board member of the Heffter Research Institute, and the founder of the McKenna Academy of Natural Philosophy (2019). He has spent a career studying these plants as a scientist.

What HMR shares with him

He was unafraid to explore the limits of the human psyche, and he trusted his own experience of reality over what society told him to think. He did it his way and shared what he found. In HMR's terms that is living true to your own model of reality.

These substances are illegal in most places. HMR's view is that they deserve careful, responsible, legal research, and the McKenna brothers' work is part of why that research now exists.

SA

Sri Aurobindo

Born 15 August 1872 in Calcutta, India. Died 5 December 1950 in Pondicherry.

An Indian philosopher, poet, and yogi. He studied at King's College, Cambridge, became a leader of India's independence movement, and after his acquittal in the Alipore trial of 1909 turned wholly to spiritual work.

Life and work

  • Moved to Pondicherry in 1910 and founded the Sri Aurobindo Ashram there in 1926 with Mirra Alfassa, known as the Mother.
  • Major works: The Life Divine, The Synthesis of Yoga, and the epic poem Savitri, about 24,000 lines long.
  • Nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature (1943) and for Peace (1950).

His central idea

Integral Yoga: the aim is to transform the whole person, mind, life, and body, and not to escape the world. He saw evolution as consciousness unfolding toward a higher state he called the supermind, which would change the physical body itself.

What HMR shares with him

We know his work less well than the others here. What drew us is that he took the link between mind and body seriously enough to expect consciousness to change the physical body. HMR holds the same.

BS

Baruch Spinoza

Born 24 November 1632 in Amsterdam. Died 21 February 1677 in The Hague.

A Dutch philosopher from Amsterdam's Portuguese-Jewish community, which expelled him in 1656, at the age of 23, for his views. He earned his living grinding lenses and turned down a university chair to keep his freedom of thought.

Life and work

  • Theological-Political Treatise (1670), published anonymously, caused an uproar across Europe.
  • Ethics, his chief work, was published in 1677, after his death.

His central idea

There is one infinite substance, which he called God or Nature. Everything that exists is a way that one substance is. Mind and body are the same thing understood in two ways.

What HMR shares with him

One reality, with mind and body as two sides of it, reasoned step by step: that is the shape of HMR. He also paid the price of holding a model of reality that his community would not accept, and held it anyway.

The science of fascia

The Fascia Research Society

The Fascia Research Society is a nonprofit of scientists and clinicians devoted to "researching the functions of fasciae". It supports fascia research, education, and scientific collaboration, runs webinars and a journal, and is the home of the international Fascia Research Congress.

HMR has no affiliation with the society. We praise its work. In HMR's view these researchers and practitioners are the tip of humanity's spear on fascia: a small group who are well ahead of the curve, and the people to look to as this field grows.

Channels we watch

Four channels that taught us a great deal

Science

Veritasium

Made by Derek Muller, who holds a doctorate in physics education research from the University of Sydney. Since 2011 the channel has taken on the ideas in science that people most often get wrong, with experiments and interviews. It is a model of how to teach in a way that is fun, creative, and exciting. We have learned a great deal from it.

YouTubeveritasium.com
Physics and astronomy

PBS Space Time

Hosted and written since 2015 by Matt O'Dowd, an astrophysicist at Lehman College of the City University of New York. It is rigorous about the mathematics and still watchable for anyone curious. Its community on Discord gave HMR some strong criticism early on, and that criticism helped us get here.

PBS Space TimeOn YouTube
Mathematics

3Blue1Brown

Made by Grant Sanderson, a Stanford mathematics graduate, since 2015. He teaches higher mathematics through animation, so that you watch an idea being invented instead of being handed a formula. To us it is the best mathematics channel there is.

3blue1brown.comOn YouTube
Health

Huberman Lab

Hosted by Andrew Huberman, associate professor of neurobiology and ophthalmology at Stanford. Since 2021 he has put long conversations about health science in front of the public, where they can be examined. Our views differ from those of many of his guests, and some scientists have criticized claims made on the show. The body of material it has opened up is very large and very useful.

In short

HMR grew out of what others had already done. Sadhguru's yoga, Alan Watts bringing East and West together, Terence McKenna's fearless exploring, Sri Aurobindo's work on mind and body, and Spinoza's one substance each run in the same direction. The Fascia Research Society leads the science of fascia, and a few channels taught us physics, mathematics, and health along the way. None of them is affiliated with HMR or has endorsed it.
