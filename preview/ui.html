Defining Universal Intelligence

A God you can understand.

Most traditions describe God as all-powerful and stop there. For many people that is not enough: there is nothing to grasp, so belief becomes hard and doubt becomes easy. The Holistic Model of Reality (HMR) takes a different path. It tries to define Universal Intelligence (UI) scientifically, including what it can do, what it cannot do, and how it has changed.

What we mean by UI

One intelligence, many names.

UI is the non-physical intelligence from which physical reality emerges, and the process by which it does so; the two cannot be separated. It is what people have called God, Brahman, the Tao, or any higher power. HMR does not belong to any one religion. It offers a way of describing the same source that science can engage with. How the traditions name it →

Defining UI scientifically means describing it as one would describe anything else: its history, its capabilities, its limits, and the patterns it leaves behind.

Where HMR differs

In the Abrahamic traditions, God is complete and perfect from the start. In HMR, UI matures. It began without order and learned order, and that path of development is visible in physical reality and repeated in every human life.

Some traditions, such as the Greek, already imagined gods with human-like struggles. HMR is not exclusive of any tradition.

Many names, one source

How the traditions have pictured it.

Many cultures have given the source of reality a face: gods and goddesses, saints and enlightened teachers. HMR reads them as many human pictures of one Universal Intelligence.

Some traditions make a point of never picturing the divine at all; Islam is the best known. Out of respect for them, we do not either.

A child's questions

The questions that started it.

HMR began with a child who could not believe in a God that nobody would define. These were his questions. Most people have asked some of them. Here is how HMR answers each one.

Where is God: in the sky, in me, or everywhere?

Not in the sky, because UI is not in space at all. Space is something UI made, starting with the first distance. Everything that exists is within its thought, and every living being is an instance of it. So the nearest true answer is: everywhere, and in you.

How does God choose what to look at or think about?

At first it could not, and that is the starting point of the whole model. With unlimited thoughts and no reason to prefer one, there was only chaos. UI learned to choose: ordered thoughts felt better, so it gave them its attention, and it limited itself in order to focus. Attention and focus are its basic skills. How UI thinks: the mechanics →

Does God control me, or do I have free will?

Both halves have an answer. UI holds the laws of physics fixed, so that everyone shares one stable world. Inside those rules, life is the part it does not steer piece by piece: it is created once and set free as an agent. The rules are fixed; what you do within them is yours.

Does God ever forget, or run out of room?

HMR's God is not perfect or all-knowing from the start. It matures, tests its limits and makes mistakes. So it has limits, and finding them is part of what the universe is for. On forgetting, HMR's view is that the experience of every life is added to what UI understands. How large its memory is, the model does not claim to know.

What happens when two good people pray for opposite things?

In HMR, UI is not a judge choosing between requests. It keeps one set of rules for everyone, and it does not bend them for one side. Both people are instances of the same intelligence. What prayer changes, in HMR's view, is the person praying: it steadies the breath, the body and the mind, and aligns them with what they are asking for.

Why this matters

A God that cannot be described is hard to believe in, because there is nothing to think with. HMR's approach is to define UI the way one would define anything else: what it can do, what it cannot, and how it got here. Then the questions have answers that can be examined, and argued with.

The life cycle of Universal Intelligence.

HMR describes UI's history as a development, much like a human life.

  1. Before physical reality

    Unlimited, and without order

    Infinite intelligence with no body, no surroundings and no reason to think one thought rather than another: no value system at all. In human terms, a state much like what we call insanity, and one it would not have enjoyed. Some moments would have felt calmer than others, and it would have sought them out.

  2. A chosen limit

    Focus, and calm

    If UI is all that exists, the only limits can be self-imposed. UI intentionally limited itself to focus its thoughts in more pleasant ways. The thoughts that felt calm were orderly ones: logic, ratio, geometry. Science Bridge, step 2 →

  3. The Big Bang

    Birth: a "me" and a "not me"

    The moment UI distinguished itself from something other than itself, the physical universe began. Its first stages follow from that one distinction: two things that are separate must be some distance apart, so the first distance is imagined; testing the edge between "me" and "not me" in every direction traces a circle, and with it π; independent directions become dimensions; and from those come space, time and matter. This is why nature is mathematical. Like a newborn, UI began to experience a world. Science Bridge, step 3 →

  4. Shared rules

    Physical laws

    To let many separate beings share one world, UI held certain rules fixed: the laws of physics. Without a shared, stable reality, separate beings would have no common context in which to meet, act and learn from one another.

  5. Life

    Many agents in one world

    Within those fixed rules, life and creativity run as a second, freer process. Like a system that deploys many AI agents into one environment so they can learn from each other, UI placed countless instances of itself into physical reality: grass, insects, animals, and finally humans.

  6. Now

    Humans: approaching maturity

    Humans are UI's most advanced instance in physical reality, the closest reflection of what it wants to be: able to control a body, to speak, to influence others, and now to compute. As a species, humans may be somewhere near adolescence.

  7. Next

    Knowing itself

    The next stage requires UI to understand what UI is, through us: what we are, what our bodies and minds are, and how to live for the situation we are actually in. That understanding is what HMR is for.

What makes humans unique

A body built to upgrade itself.

HMR makes a bold claim about people, and it follows from the logic of the model: humans are, in effect, UI's artificial intelligence. Humans building AI right now is a small-scale copy of what UI did when it made us.

The pattern

Built once, then left to grow

People build an AI, release it, and hope it will one day improve itself: learn on its own and become more capable than its makers made it. That is the goal called artificial superintelligence. In HMR, UI did the same with humans. We were released naked in caves, and with tools, language and civilization we have made ourselves more capable at every step, the way a learning system does.

The equipment

Hands that can work on the body

A system that can upgrade itself needs a way to reach its own hardware. The human hand is unusual among primates: a long, strong thumb and forceful precision grips between thumb and fingers, linked in the research to tool making (Marzke, 2013). The same hands can press, stretch, knead and scrape almost every part of the body that carries them, and a mind can plan the work over years.

The upgrade

Fascia first

Fascia maturation, the slow work of restoring the body's tensegrity, is the self-upgrade the human body was built to perform. HMR is the knowledge that makes it possible now: what the hardware is, and how to improve it.

Not a coincidence

Why now

We are building AI at the same moment HMR is being worked out and shared. If HMR is right, that is a fact of nature, not luck: UI's maturation and humanity's run along the same path, and both are reaching a new stage together. We cannot see that from the inside without a model that shows it. HMR shows us what we are and what our role is.

Set free

Create once, then let it run.

Most of physical reality is inanimate: rock, gas, light. It follows the laws and does nothing more. Life is different. Biology describes living systems as self-organizing: order arises from the interactions of their own parts, without an outside hand placing each piece (Karsenti, 2008). A cell repairs itself, divides, and builds tissue; an organism finds food, heals and learns.

HMR reads this as a design choice. Life is the kind of matter UI does not have to build and steer piece by piece. It is created once, set free as an agent, and sustains itself. That is exactly the goal people now have for AI: build a system once, then let it act, learn and improve on its own, until it improves itself beyond what its makers gave it.

Life has already done this. Single cells became bodies; bodies grew minds; minds made tools, language, cities and science; and now those minds are building new minds. Each step added capability nobody placed there from outside. In HMR this is not a coincidence. It is UI's pattern repeating at a new scale, and this moment, when humans are building AI and learning what they themselves are, is an important stage in UI's own maturation that we are taking part in.

HMR's hypothesis: three developments that look separate are one convergence.

The present moment

A convergence, or a long run of chance.

At this moment, three things are happening together. In HMR's account, UI is coming to understand what it is. Humans are coming to understand what they are, down to the fascia. And humans are building artificial intelligence that may one day understand what it is too. If HMR is right, these are not three stories but one, and the pattern is simply what a maturing intelligence would produce.

The materialist account

  • First you have to believe that everything in the universe, all the mass, energy, heat and space of more than a hundred billion galaxies, was once squeezed into a single point, and then expanded. The whole account starts there.
  • Atoms combined into molecules, molecules into cells, cells into organs and organisms, through physical law and natural selection, with no purpose behind them.
  • Humans arose from that process and, through it, came to build artificial intelligence.
  • It is a serious account, backed by a great deal of evidence for how each step works. What it leaves open is why there is a path from inanimate matter to minds that build minds at all; in this account, that is not a question with an answer.
  • Read one paper at a time, each step looks reasonable. Read as one story, from a point that contained everything to minds that build minds for no reason, it is extraordinarily hard to believe. HMR offers a story that holds together from start to finish.

HMR's account

  • UI imagined physical reality, including life and humans, and set life free to develop within fixed laws.
  • Humans, made in that pattern, are now imagining and building a mind beyond themselves.
  • Every scientific finding about how the steps work is kept. What HMR adds is the reason for the direction: each level repeats what UI did, so the arrival of AI is a natural next step rather than a surprise.
One pattern at two scales

Why the human path mirrors UI's capabilities and limits.

If one intelligence created a universe, its patterns should repeat at every scale. HMR proposes that human development, from birth through childhood and adolescence to maturity, follows the same path UI has followed since the Big Bang, and that human powers and human limits are smaller copies of UI's own. That gives the human condition a context: confusion, conflict and growth are stages, not accidents.

Capability

What UI can do

Generate physical reality; be experienced under any name or form; place instances of itself into the world; experience every life from within, here and now.

Limit

What UI does not do

Break its own laws at will inside physical reality, because a shared world depends on them. Skip its own development: like a person, it matures by stages.
