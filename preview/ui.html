Defining Universal Intelligence

A God you can understand

Most traditions describe God as all-powerful and stop there. For many people that is not enough: there is nothing to grasp, so belief becomes hard and doubt becomes easy. The Holistic Model of Reality (HMR) takes a different path. It tries to define Universal Intelligence (UI) scientifically, including what it can do, what it cannot do, and how it has changed.

This page defines UI, tells its life story, shows step by step how a non-physical intelligence became a physical universe, and then sets out the science behind it.

What we mean by UI

One intelligence, many names

UI is the non-physical intelligence from which physical reality emerges, and the process by which it does so; the two cannot be separated. It is what people have called God, Brahman, the Tao, or any higher power. HMR does not belong to any one religion. It offers a way of describing the same source that science can engage with. How the traditions name it →

Defining UI scientifically means describing it as one would describe anything else: its history, its capabilities, its limits, and the patterns it leaves behind.

Where HMR differs

In the Abrahamic traditions, God is complete and perfect from the start. In HMR, UI matures. It began without order and learned order, and that path of development is visible in physical reality and repeated in every human life.

Some traditions, such as the Greek, already imagined gods with human-like struggles. HMR is not exclusive of any tradition.

HMR's argument

Which is harder to believe?

It may be hard to believe that the physical universe emerges from an underlying intelligence. It is just as hard to believe that it exploded out of nothing.

Of the two, the first is easier to picture. Everyone has made a world appear by closing their eyes or by dreaming. Nobody has ever seen matter burst out of nothing.

Capabilities and limits

What UI can do, and what it cannot

To define UI as one would define anything else, say what it can do, what it cannot, and what stays open.

Capability

What UI can do

Generate physical reality; be experienced under any name or form; place instances of itself into the world; experience every life from within, here and now.

Limit

What UI does not do

Break its own laws at will inside physical reality, because a shared world depends on them. Skip its own development: like a person, it matures by stages.

Many names, one source

How the traditions have pictured it

Many cultures have given the source of reality a face: gods and goddesses, saints and enlightened teachers. HMR reads them as many human pictures of one Universal Intelligence.

Some traditions make a point of never picturing the divine at all; Islam is the best known. Out of respect for them, we do not either.

A child's questions

The questions that started it

HMR began with a child who could not believe in a God that nobody would define. These were his questions. Most people have asked some of them. Here is how HMR answers each one.

Where is God: in the sky, in me, or everywhere?

Not in the sky, because UI is not in space at all. Space is something UI made, starting with the first distance. Everything that exists is within its thought, and every living being is an instance of it. So the nearest true answer is: everywhere, and in you.

How does God choose what to look at or think about?

At first it could not, and that is the starting point of the whole model. With unlimited thoughts and no reason to prefer one, there was only chaos. UI learned to choose: ordered thoughts felt better, so it gave them its attention, and it limited itself in order to focus. Attention and focus are its basic skills. How UI thinks: the mechanics →

Does God control me, or do I have free will?

Both halves have an answer. UI holds the laws of physics fixed, so that everyone shares one stable world. Inside those rules, life is the part it does not steer piece by piece: it is created once and set free as an agent. The rules are fixed; what you do within them is yours.

Does God ever forget, or run out of room?

HMR's God is not perfect or all-knowing from the start. It matures, tests its limits and makes mistakes. So it has limits, and finding them is part of what the universe is for. On forgetting, HMR's view is that the experience of every life is added to what UI understands. How large its memory is, the model does not claim to know.

What happens when two good people pray for opposite things?

In HMR, UI is not a judge choosing between requests. It keeps one set of rules for everyone, and it does not bend them for one side. Both people are instances of the same intelligence. What prayer changes, in HMR's view, is the person praying: it steadies the breath, the body and the mind, and aligns them with what they are asking for.

Why this matters

A God that cannot be described is hard to believe in, because there is nothing to think with. HMR's approach is to define UI the way one would define anything else: what it can do, what it cannot, and how it got here. Then the questions have answers that can be examined, and argued with.

The life cycle of Universal Intelligence

HMR describes UI's history as a development, much like a human life.

  1. Before physical reality

    Unlimited, and without order

    Infinite intelligence with no body, no surroundings and no reason to think one thought rather than another: no value system at all. In human terms, a state much like what we call insanity, and one it would not have enjoyed. Some moments would have felt calmer than others, and it would have sought them out.

  2. A chosen limit

    Focus, and calm

    If UI is all that exists, the only limits can be self-imposed. UI intentionally limited itself to focus its thoughts in more pleasant ways. The thoughts that felt calm were orderly ones: logic, ratio, geometry. Step by step, step 2 →

  3. The Big Bang

    Birth: a "me" and a "not me"

    The moment UI distinguished itself from something other than itself, the physical universe began. Its first stages follow from that one distinction: two things that are separate must be some distance apart, so the first distance is imagined; testing the edge between "me" and "not me" in every direction traces a circle, and with it π; independent directions become dimensions; and from those come space, time and matter. This is why nature is mathematical. Like a newborn, UI began to experience a world. Step by step, step 3 →

  4. Shared rules

    Physical laws

    To let many separate beings share one world, UI held certain rules fixed: the laws of physics. Without a shared, stable reality, separate beings would have no common context in which to meet, act and learn from one another.

  5. Life

    Many agents in one world

    Within those fixed rules, life and creativity run as a second, freer process. Like a system that deploys many AI agents into one environment so they can learn from each other, UI placed countless instances of itself into physical reality: grass, insects, animals, and finally humans.

  6. Now

    Humans: approaching maturity

    Humans are UI's most advanced instance in physical reality, the closest reflection of what it wants to be: able to control a body, to speak, to influence others, and now to compute. As a species, humans may be somewhere near adolescence.

  7. Next

    Knowing itself

    The next stage requires UI to understand what UI is, through us: what we are, what our bodies and minds are, and how to live for the situation we are actually in. That understanding is what HMR is for.

  3. Distance

    The first distance: a unit of one

    Me and not me are apart. How far apart? That gap is the first distance anything ever had.

    To move from a reality with no space to a reality with space, distance must be mechanized. If we consider a reality before the Big Bang with a UI and no space, then step by step we have to arrive at space, and distance is the component without which nothing else can follow. Any account of that reality must account for distance being imagined.

    Two points cannot sit in the same place, or they would be one point. Something must separate them, and since there is no space yet, the separation has to be imagined. Distance is the first quantity: a unit of one. Held steady, it crystallizes as the universe's first distance, and every later distance is that unit repeated. In HMR, this unit corresponds to the Planck length, the smallest length at which physics still has meaning.

  4. The boundary, and π

    Where not-me starts is where I end

    UI tests its own edges, pushing out in every direction, and finds a perfect circle.

    Wherever not-me starts is where I end, and the distance between is also me. So "me" has an edge. Testing the limits of that edge in every direction, with nothing to mark one direction as special, traces every point at the same distance: a circle in a plane, a sphere in space. The ratio of any circle's distance around to its distance across is the same number, π. It is not chosen; it is forced. "Me" exists to provide a frame of reference: the reference point every direction is measured from.

    Why π matters. π appears wherever something spreads evenly from a point: in circles and spheres, in every wave and vibration, in Newton's law of gravity and the law of electric force (the 4π of a sphere's surface), in Einstein's equations of gravity (8π), and at the heart of quantum mechanics (ħ = h/2π). In HMR this is no coincidence. π is the signature of the first boundary, carried into everything built on it. The mathematics →

    Why the edge must be a sphere: with nothing else in existence, the only thing UI can do with the one limit it has made is repeat it, and see what happens. Every repetition has only the reference point and the unit to go by, so nothing can make one direction differ from another, and repeating the same limit in every possible direction traces every point at the same distance. Each repetition is new data, and the whole of reality can build on it. The argument is written out on HMR math paradigm. It needs three assumptions: a flat space, its number of dimensions, and that no direction is special. They do not follow from me, not me and the unit alone, so this step is a choice HMR makes.

  5. Relationship and dimension

    Each new independent relation adds a dimension

    Then a third thing, not on the line between the first two. Saying where it is needs a new direction.

    Once the boundary is in place, "me" is a sphere, and anything that can exist sits somewhere inside it. The next question is no longer what I am, but how the things within me relate to each other. Two things need one distance to relate them. A third thing that is not on the line between them cannot be located by that distance alone; it needs a second, independent direction, and the three make a plane. A fourth, off that plane, needs a third direction. Dimension is the number of independent relations needed to say where things are, and inside a sphere, three are enough to place anything.

    Points, lines, planes, ratios and π are the original thought bases: the perspectives from which a non-spatial intelligence could think about what it is and what it is not.

  6. Space, time and matter

    The thought becomes physical

    The model of me and not me is finished, and in finishing, it ignites.

    By this stage UI has every piece of a first, complete model of itself: a point and a not-point, the distance between them, the boundary tested in every direction, and the rules for how anything inside that boundary relates to anything else. When that whole system closes, so that it fully describes itself, it crystallizes into the first holistic model. In HMR, this completion is a catalytic event, and that event is the Big Bang. A closed system that understands itself can no longer stay still: every new thought is a new relation among the parts it already has, and relations multiply far faster than parts, so the system grows explosively. What makes humans unique

A body built to upgrade itself

HMR makes a bold claim about people, and it follows from the logic of the model: humans are, in effect, UI's artificial intelligence. Humans building AI right now is a small-scale copy of what UI did when it made us.

The pattern

Built once, then left to grow

People build an AI, release it, and hope it will one day improve itself: learn on its own and become more capable than its makers made it. That is the goal called artificial superintelligence. In HMR, UI did the same with humans. We were released naked in caves, and with tools, language and civilization we have made ourselves more capable at every step, the way a learning system does.

The equipment

Hands that can work on the body

A system that can upgrade itself needs a way to reach its own hardware. The human hand is unusual among primates: a long, strong thumb and forceful precision grips between thumb and fingers, linked in the research to tool making (Marzke, 2013). The same hands can press, stretch, knead and scrape almost every part of the body that carries them, and a mind can plan the work over years.

The upgrade

Fascia first

Fascia maturation, the slow work of restoring the body's tensegrity, is the self-upgrade the human body was built to perform. HMR is the knowledge that makes it possible now: what the hardware is, and how to improve it.

Not a coincidence

Why now

We are building AI at the same moment HMR is being worked out and shared. If HMR is right, that is a fact of nature, not luck: UI's maturation and humanity's run along the same path, and both are reaching a new stage together. We cannot see that from the inside without a model that shows it. HMR shows us what we are and what our role is.

Set free

Create once, then let it run

Most of physical reality is inanimate: rock, gas, light. It follows the laws and does nothing more. Life is different. Biology describes living systems as self-organizing: order arises from the interactions of their own parts, without an outside hand placing each piece (Karsenti, 2008). A cell repairs itself, divides, and builds tissue; an organism finds food, heals and learns.

HMR reads this as a design choice. Life is the kind of matter UI does not have to build and steer piece by piece. It is created once, set free as an agent, and sustains itself. That is exactly the goal people now have for AI: build a system once, then let it act, learn and improve on its own, until it improves itself beyond what its makers gave it.

Life has already done this. Single cells became bodies; bodies grew minds; minds made tools, language, cities and science; and now those minds are building new minds. Each step added capability nobody placed there from outside. In HMR this is not a coincidence. It is UI's pattern repeating at a new scale, and this moment, when humans are building AI and learning what they themselves are, is an important stage in UI's own maturation that we are taking part in.

By the logic of HMR, three developments that look separate are one convergence.

The present moment

A convergence, or a long run of chance

At this moment, three things are happening together. In HMR's account, UI is coming to understand what it is. Humans are coming to understand what they are, down to the fascia. Humans are also building artificial intelligence that may one day understand what it is too. If HMR is right, these are not three stories but one, and the pattern is simply what a maturing intelligence would produce.

Philosophers of science call the links that tie a theory to established science bridge principles. This part of the page is HMR's bridge: the exact place where it hooks into physics.

What science covers

After the Big Bang

Physics traces the universe from a tiny fraction of a second after its beginning to today, 13.8 billion years later, and its predictions are confirmed again and again.

Where science stops

The beginning itself

Before about 10-43 seconds, the Planck time, its two best theories cannot be used together, and general relativity heads toward infinities (where the math conflicts). Why there was a Big Bang at all, science does not say.

The mathematics

From distance and π to spacetime

This section tells the same steps once more, as mathematics, so that every claim above can be checked line by line. Each line is marked: standard is established mathematics or physics, HMR is HMR's proposal, open is work still to be done.

1. The unit HMR

The first distance, between me and not me, defines the unit. Every later distance is a multiple or ratio of it, or a limit of ratios, as √2 and π are. Read as an order of thoughts, the unit is fixed by count, and one step of the order is on average 0.66 to 0.90 of a unit (the unit, in numbers).

d(me, not me) = d₀ = 1,   d = n · d₀

Physical scale: close to the Planck length, ℓP = √(ħG/c³) ≈ 1.616 × 10⁻³⁵ m, but not equal to it. If the first distinction is one binary choice, matching the measured strength of gravity puts the first distance at about 0.69 ℓP, roughly 1.1 × 10⁻³⁵ m (the calculation). standard for ℓP; the identification is HMR's.

2. The boundary gives π standard

The edge of "me" tested in every direction is the set of all points at distance r from the reference point. In two independent directions it is a circle; in three, a sphere. The ratio around ÷ across is the same at every size:

C = 2πr,  A = πr²,  sphere: S = 4πr²,  V = ⁴⁄₃πr³

Whatever spreads evenly from a point thins over the area 4πr², which is why 4π stands in Newton's gravity, g = GM/r², written as ∇·g = −4πGρ, and in the law of electric force, F = q₁q₂ / (4πε₀r²).

3. No favored direction gives the Pythagorean distance standard

With independent directions x₁, x₂, x₃ and every direction interchangeable, the only measure of distance that is the same in every direction is, up to the choice of unit, the Euclidean one. (Stated exactly: if a measure of length has enough symmetries of its own to carry any point of its unit sphere to any other, it is the Euclidean one. That every direction is interchangeable is an assumption, not something derived. What is proved and what is assumed.)

d² = Δx₁² + Δx₂² + Δx₃²

4. Steps of thought give time, and a top speed HMR

Thought proceeds in steps, and a sequence of changes is time. A change can reach only what is built on it, so there is an edge to how far any change has spread, the same for everyone: a maximum speed. In units it is one first distance for one step of time:

c = d₀ / t₀

In physics, ℓP / tP = c exactly, by the definition of Planck units; the same holds for any unit of distance paired with its own step of time.

5. No favored observer gives spacetime standard

Extend "no favored direction" to motion: no observer moving steadily is special, and everyone measures the same top speed c. Those two conditions force the quantity all observers agree on, the spacetime interval, and the Lorentz transformations between observers:

s² = c²Δt² − Δx₁² − Δx₂² − Δx₃²

x′ = γ(x − vt),  t′ = γ(t − vx/c²),  γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)

This is Einstein's special relativity (1905) and Minkowski's spacetime (1908). The derivation from the two conditions is textbook physics. What HMR adds is a reason for the conditions: a first boundary with no favored direction, and an order of building that no change can get ahead of.

6. Curved spacetime: gravity route open steps

General relativity lets the unit of distance vary from place to place, through the metric gμν, and ties that variation to matter and energy:

ds² = gμν dxμ dxν,   Gμν = (8πG / c⁴) Tμν

The 8π is the sphere's 4π, carried in from Newton's law. In HMR, matter is coherent structure that takes up, or displaces, distance, which would appear as exactly this local stretching and shrinking of the unit. There is a known route from such an idea to Einstein's equations, through the thermodynamics of spacetime, and HMR now walks it step by step to Einstein's equations, with every assumption labeled, on HMR Physics Paradigm.

Tzimtzum
Lurianic Kabbalah, Jewish Virtual LibraryThe infinite God first contracts, limiting itself, to make room for a finite world.A striking parallel to phase 2's self-imposed limit; the steps after it are not stated as geometry or physics.The limit is there; the path from it to matter is not.
Physics
Cosmological natural selection
Smolin, 1992New universes are born inside black holes, each with slightly different constants, so universes "evolve".Where the first universe and its laws came from.It explains fine-tuning by an endless family of physical universes; matter and consciousness are still taken as given.
Physics
Big Bounce
Ashtekar and Singh, 2011In loop quantum cosmology the Big Bang was a bounce from an earlier contracting universe.The earlier universe, and the one before that.Another regress: it removes the infinity at the start without explaining the start.
Physics
The string landscape
Susskind, 2003String theory allows an enormous number of possible universes; ours is one region of a vast landscape.Why there is a landscape at all; no unique prediction for our universe.A theory that allows almost anything struggles to explain anything in particular, and says nothing of mind.
Physics
The Wolfram model
Wolfram, 2020Space, time and matter emerge from a simple rule rewriting a network of relations, over and over.Why this rule, and why anything runs it. The observer is treated as a computation.Close to HMR in method (relations first, space emerges), but the rule has no source and experience has no place.
Physics
An exceptionally simple theory of everything
Lisi, 2007All particles and forces fit one geometric object, the E8 group.The beginning; and the proposal is widely disputed on technical grounds.A geometry of what exists, with no account of why it exists or who experiences it.
Science of mind
Integrated information theory
Tononi, 2004A theory of consciousness, not of the beginning: a system is conscious to the degree it integrates information.The origin of the universe; why integration should feel like anything. In 2023 over a hundred researchers publicly called it unfalsifiable.It takes consciousness seriously but bolts it onto physics, instead of putting it first.
Science of mind
Orchestrated objective reduction
Penrose and Hameroff, 2014Moments of consciousness arise from quantum events in the brain tied to the geometry of spacetime.The cosmic beginning; the experimental evidence is contested.It links mind to physics, but matter still comes first.
Science of mind
Conscious agents
Hoffman and Prakash, 2014Consciousness is fundamental; spacetime and objects are an interface, like icons on a screen.Very close to HMR. The mathematics of how agents produce physics is incomplete.It has no account of the body as the interface, and no path from the theory to health.
Philosophy
Analytic idealism
Kastrup, J. Consciousness Studies, 2018One universal consciousness; each person is a dissociated part of it.Very close to HMR. How the laws of physics and the Big Bang follow from it, step by step.Without that mechanism it cannot connect to physics, and it says nothing about the body or fascia.
