The Science Bridge · for skeptics

"You want me to believe in God. Where is the science?"

A fair question, answered in one page. Science explains very well what happened after the Big Bang. The Holistic Model of Reality (HMR) adds what science does not attempt: a step-by-step account of how a non-physical intelligence became a physical universe, with no step that breaks logic.

Philosophers of science call the links that tie a theory to established science bridge principles. This page is HMR's bridge: the exact place where it hooks into physics.

The rest of the site covers the detail: the gaps in physics, Universal Intelligence, health.

What science covers

After the Big Bang

Physics traces the universe from a tiny fraction of a second after its beginning to today, 13.8 billion years later, and its predictions are confirmed again and again.

Where science stops

The beginning itself

Before about 10-43 seconds, the Planck time, its two best theories cannot be used together, and general relativity heads toward infinities (where the math conflicts). Why there was a Big Bang at all, science does not say.

What HMR adds

What the Big Bang was, and why it happened

Traditions say God created the world. HMR goes further and defines the segue: what existed first, why it changed, what the Big Bang actually was, why it happened, and the first thoughts that became geometry, distance and the physical universe.

From non-physical to physical

The transition, step by step.

Each step follows from the one before it, and each is told twice: as the story of UI coming to understand "me" versus "not me", and as the reasoning behind it. Step through the figure, or tap any step.

Science asks people who believe in God to prove it, and no one can, for or against. What can be done is to go thought by thought: if before the Big Bang there was a God and no space, then what steps must happen, in order, to arrive at space? That is what follows. It is a story that makes better sense than the scientific picture alone, contains all of science, contradicts none of it, and is more useful to live by. Ask what the world would look like if everyone believed it, and the answer is a better one. That combination is the reason to take it seriously.

Step 1. Each dot is a thought. Unlimited intelligence: every thought at once.

  1. Phase 1: before physical reality

    An unlimited intelligence, with every thought at once

    Imagine being everything, with nothing around you, and every idea you could ever have arriving at once, with no reason to hold one rather than another.

    Before physical reality there is only Universal Intelligence (UI): no space, no time, no matter, and no limits. In the figure, each dot is an idea or a thought, all of them racing at once. In human terms that state is close to what we would call insanity. It would not have been a pleasant experience.

  2. Phase 2: a chosen limit

    A limit, and it is self-imposed

    Like a person overwhelmed by noise who chooses to focus on one calm thing, UI draws its thoughts in.

    Something unlimited can change only by taking on a limit. If UI is all that exists, the only limits can be self-imposed. UI intentionally limited itself to focus its thoughts in more pleasant ways. In the figure the thoughts gather into a smaller, calmer place. The pleasant thoughts were the orderly ones, ideas that lead to further ideas, as a framework does. Orderly thoughts make patterns, patterns make prediction possible, and predictability is a form of stability.

    This is the birth of mathematics. In HMR, mathematics is the ordered pattern of thought within a Universal Intelligence that exists among thoughts without order. HMR math paradigm →

  3. Step 3

    The Big Bang, in its first stages

    If UI is the only thing that exists, the question "what am I?" is a deep paradox. UI is everything, but it does not yet know that. To ask the question at all, it has to imagine something it is not. In HMR, the Big Bang is what follows from that act of imagination: the first stages of UI coming to understand itself by contrast with what it is not. Steps 3.1 to 3.5 are those stages, in order.

  4. The first framework

    Me, and not me

    UI's first focused question: what am I, and what am I not?

    To focus at all, UI needs a first framework, and the first framework is identity. To know what I am, I must tell myself apart from what I am not. The smallest possible distinction needs exactly two things: a point that is me, and a point imagined as not me.

  5. Distance

    The first distance: a unit of one

    Me and not me are apart. How far apart? That gap is the first distance anything ever had.

    To move from a reality with no space to a reality with space, distance must be mechanized. If we consider a reality before the Big Bang with a UI and no space, then step by step we have to arrive at space, and distance is the component without which nothing else can follow. Any account of that reality must account for distance being imagined.

    Two points cannot sit in the same place, or they would be one point. Something must separate them, and since there is no space yet, the separation has to be imagined. Distance is the first quantity: a unit of one. Held steady, it crystallizes as the universe's first distance, and every later distance is that unit repeated. HMR proposes that this unit may correspond to the Planck length, the smallest length at which physics still has meaning.

  6. The boundary, and π

    Where not-me starts is where I end

    UI tests its own edges, pushing out in every direction, and finds a perfect circle.

    Wherever not-me starts is where I end, and the distance between is also me. So "me" has an edge. Testing the limits of that edge in every direction, with nothing to mark one direction as special, traces every point at the same distance: a circle in a plane, a sphere in space. The ratio of any circle's distance around to its distance across is the same number, π. It is not chosen; it is forced. "Me" exists to provide a frame of reference: the reference point every direction is measured from.

    Why π matters. π appears wherever something spreads evenly from a point: in circles and spheres, in every wave and vibration, in Newton's law of gravity and the law of electric force (the 4π of a sphere's surface), in Einstein's equations of gravity (8π), and at the heart of quantum mechanics (ħ = h/2π). In HMR this is no coincidence. π is the signature of the first boundary, carried into everything built on it. The mathematics →

    Why the edge must be a sphere: with nothing else in existence, the only thing UI can do with the one limit it has made is repeat it, and see what happens. Every repetition has only the reference point and the unit to go by, so nothing can make one direction differ from another, and repeating the same limit in every possible direction traces every point at the same distance. Each repetition is new data, and the whole of reality can build on it. The argument is written out on HMR math paradigm. It needs three assumptions: a flat space, its number of dimensions, and that no direction is special. They do not follow from me, not me and the unit alone, so this step is a choice HMR makes, and the page says what speaks for it.

  7. Relationship and dimension

    Each new independent relation adds a dimension

    Then a third thing, not on the line between the first two. Saying where it is needs a new direction.

    Once the boundary is in place, "me" is a sphere, and anything that can exist sits somewhere inside it. The next question is no longer what I am, but how the things within me relate to each other. Two things need one distance to relate them. A third thing that is not on the line between them cannot be located by that distance alone; it needs a second, independent direction, and the three make a plane. A fourth, off that plane, needs a third direction. Dimension is the number of independent relations needed to say where things are, and inside a sphere, three are enough to place anything.

    Points, lines, planes, ratios and π are the original thought bases: the perspectives from which a non-spatial intelligence could think about what it is and what it is not.

  8. Space, time and matter

    The thought becomes physical

    The model of me and not me is finished, and in finishing, it ignites.

    By this stage UI has every piece of a first, complete model of itself: a point and a not-point, the distance between them, the boundary tested in every direction, and the rules for how anything inside that boundary relates to anything else. When that whole system closes, so that it fully describes itself, it crystallizes into the first holistic model. HMR proposes that this completion is a catalytic event, and that the event is the Big Bang. A closed system that understands itself can no longer stay still: every new thought is a new relation among the parts it already has, and relations multiply far faster than parts, so the system grows explosively. The mathematics of that moment →

    From then on, the universe can model itself again, on top of what exploded. Thinking in steps gives time; independent directions give space; from that structure come shapes, energies and the interactions between them. The stable rules of that exploration are what we measure as the laws of physics, and from here the account and mainstream science run together. HMR proposes that later, within that remodeling, a second catalytic event made life possible: a kind of inversion, in which the system, having defined its outside fully, turns to look within and observe itself in finer detail. Life is the universe beginning to model itself from the inside.

The mathematics

From distance and π to spacetime.

This section tells the same steps a third time, as mathematics, so that every claim above can be checked line by line. Each line is marked: standard is established mathematics or physics, HMR is HMR's proposal, open is work still to be done.

1. The unit HMR

The first distance, between me and not me, defines the unit. Every later distance is a multiple or ratio of it, or a limit of ratios, as √2 and π are. Read as an order of thoughts, the unit is fixed by count, and one step of the order is on average 0.66 to 0.90 of a unit (the unit, in numbers).

d(me, not me) = d₀ = 1,   d = n · d₀

Physical scale: close to the Planck length, ℓP = √(ħG/c³) ≈ 1.616 × 10⁻³⁵ m, but not equal to it. If the first distinction is one binary choice, matching the measured strength of gravity puts the first distance at about 0.69 ℓP, roughly 1.1 × 10⁻³⁵ m (the calculation). standard for ℓP; the identification is HMR's.

2. The boundary gives π standard

The edge of "me" tested in every direction is the set of all points at distance r from the reference point. In two independent directions it is a circle; in three, a sphere. The ratio around ÷ across is the same at every size:

C = 2πr,  A = πr²,  sphere: S = 4πr²,  V = ⁴⁄₃πr³

Whatever spreads evenly from a point thins over the area 4πr², which is why 4π stands in Newton's gravity, g = GM/r², written as ∇·g = −4πGρ, and in the law of electric force, F = q₁q₂ / (4πε₀r²).

3. No favored direction gives the Pythagorean distance standard

With independent directions x₁, x₂, x₃ and every direction interchangeable, the only measure of distance that is the same in every direction is, up to the choice of unit, the Euclidean one. (Stated exactly: if a measure of length has enough symmetries of its own to carry any point of its unit sphere to any other, it is the Euclidean one. That every direction is interchangeable is an assumption, not something derived. What is proved and what is assumed.)

d² = Δx₁² + Δx₂² + Δx₃²

4. Steps of thought give time, and a top speed HMR

Thought proceeds in steps, and a sequence of changes is time. If one step can carry a change across at most one unit of distance, there is a maximum speed, the same everywhere:

c = d₀ / t₀

In physics, ℓP / tP = c exactly, by the definition of Planck units; the same holds for any unit of distance paired with its own step of time. That shows the proposal is consistent, not that it is true.

5. No favored observer gives spacetime standard

Extend "no favored direction" to motion: no observer moving steadily is special, and everyone measures the same top speed c. Those two conditions force the quantity all observers agree on, the spacetime interval, and the Lorentz transformations between observers:

s² = c²Δt² − Δx₁² − Δx₂² − Δx₃²

x′ = γ(x − vt),  t′ = γ(t − vx/c²),  γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)

This is Einstein's special relativity (1905) and Minkowski's spacetime (1908). The derivation from the two conditions is textbook physics. What HMR adds is a reason for the conditions: a first boundary with no favored direction, and a first unit of distance per step.

6. Curved spacetime: gravity route open steps

General relativity lets the unit of distance vary from place to place, through the metric gμν, and ties that variation to matter and energy:

ds² = gμν dxμ dxν,   Gμν = (8πG / c⁴) Tμν

The 8π is the sphere's 4π, carried in from Newton's law. HMR's hypothesis is that matter is coherent structure that takes up, or displaces, distance, which would appear as exactly this local stretching and shrinking of the unit. There is a known route from such an idea to Einstein's equations, through the thermodynamics of spacetime, and HMR now walks it step by step to Einstein's equations, with every assumption labeled and the three steps still open named plainly, on HMR Physics Paradigm.

Honest about the claim

What this shows, and what it does not.

It does not
  • Prove that UI exists. No step above is a measurement.
  • Replace any established result of physics.
  • Yet derive gravity. The mathematics reaches flat spacetime; curved spacetime is step 6, still open.

What it offers instead is a chain to inspect. A skeptic does not have to accept the conclusion; they only have to find the link that breaks.

It does
  • Tell one narrative that never breaks logic, from a non-physical intelligence to the physical universe.
  • Include all of science within it, unchanged, from the Big Bang onward.
  • Add what science lacks: why there is a universe, why it runs on mathematics, and what came before.
  • Bridge a God that existed and the Big Bang, instead of leaving a gap between them, and say what the Big Bang was and why it happened.
  • Lead to a system of values that fosters coherence among everyone who shares it. HMR Values →
  • Lead to practical work: a physics-based model of the healthy body that can be simulated and tested.

If no one can find a break in the framework, and it explains more than science while losing nothing science has established, then it carries more information than the scientific picture alone.

So HMR does not say "believe this". It asks you to listen to the story and decide whether it paints a better picture than the one you hold now. If it keeps holding up wherever you test it, it is probably a good one.

Related accounts

How HMR sits beside other accounts.

Any account of the beginning has to face three questions: how everything started, where matter came from, and why anyone is conscious. The physics accounts start with laws, quantum fields or space already in place, so they describe an early state rather than a true beginning, and none of them says anything about consciousness. Theology and philosophy name a source but stop before a mechanism science could check. HMR tries to answer all three in one chain.
