HMR mental health model

Psychiatry divides the mind's troubles by lists of symptoms, and the lists overlap, blur, and are applied inconsistently (the evidence). HMR proposes a different map: a description of a sound mind, and a way of saying exactly where a mind has moved away from it.

Read this first. No one involved in HMR is a clinical doctor. This is a manual in the sense that psychiatry's manual is one: an organized way of describing minds. Its basis is HMR's values and logic, not clinical trials. If you are struggling, or thinking about changing a medication, talk to a clinician you trust. You are welcome to share this site with them. It may help them to read it.

The baseline

What a sound mind does: fourteen measures in three layers

Today's manual lists what is wrong. HMR starts from what right looks like and measures the distance, the way its model of the body starts from a perfect tensegrity. The layers are ordered: how a person meets others rests on how they see themselves, and that rests on how they see reality.

This model is expected to improve. It is already a far more workable tool than what is used today. What it needs is to be taken up, tested, and rounded out by trained professionals.

A. RealityIs the person's picture of the world true?

This layer cannot be judged without an agreed account of what reality is and what we are in it. That account is what HMR supplies, and clinicians today lack it. People feel they already share one picture, because everyone senses an outside world. Without agreement on what it is, that sense is false. Whether talking to someone you cannot see counts as a symptom, for example, is decided case by case.

A1

Picture of reality

Sound: holds a picture of the world, of other people, and of what life is that matches what is the case, and updates it when shown to be wrong.

Strained: a picture far from what is the case. A wrong map leads to wrong expectations, and gaps in the map fill with fear.

A2

Reading danger

Sound: sizes up risk about right: alert when there is a threat, at ease when there is not.

Strained: sees threat everywhere, or misses real danger. HMR reads anxiety as danger signals being miscalculated.

A3

What can be changed

Sound: accepts what cannot be changed, acts on what can, and can tell the two apart.

Strained: fights what cannot be moved, or gives up on what could be. The old Serenity Prayer is a fair test of this one.

A4

Sense of time

Sound: feels there is enough time for what matters, and paces things as others do.

Strained: feels always rushed or always stalled, out of step with the people around them.

B. SelfIs the person's picture of themselves true, and are they at ease in it?
B1

Self-knowledge

Sound: their picture of themselves matches how others honestly see them: abilities, effect on people, mistakes.

Strained: a self-picture far too large or far too small. HMR weighs this more heavily than today's manuals do, because everything else is steered by it.

B2

Body and tension

Sound: a body at ease: free breath, good sleep, no chronic bracing or pain.

Strained: chronic tension, bracing, pain, poor sleep. A strained body changes how a person thinks and feels. The two maps →

B3

Depth and recovery of feeling

Sound: feels fully, including grief and tears, and comes back to balance quickly.

Strained: feels little, or is carried off by feeling and cannot come back. Anger that takes over is a signal the mind is not managing the situation the body is in.

B4

Values and agency

Sound: has values with reasons behind them, acts on them, and can set a goal and reach it.

Strained: no values, or a life lived against them; goals that are never reached. HMR Values →

B5

Perspective and appreciation

Sound: can find what is good in a situation and appreciate it, without denying what is bad.

Strained: sees only what is wrong, or cannot take pleasure in what is good.

C. OthersHow does the person meet other people?
C1

Boundaries

Sound: respects the limits other people set, and sets their own.

Strained: pushes past other people's limits, or has none of their own.

C2

Listening

Sound: listens as well as speaks, and notices whether what they say is wanted.

Strained: talks at people; keeps giving advice or ideas nobody asked for, and does not notice.

C3

Empathy

Sound: can identify with another person and understand what they are going through.

Strained: cannot put themselves in another person's place; what others are going through goes unseen or is misread.

C4

Adapting

Sound: changes approach when something is not working.

Strained: repeats what has failed, with the same people, in the same way.

C5

Kindness

Sound: is kind as a matter of course. In HMR a person in tune with what is real is kind, because others are the same intelligence in another body.

Strained: habitual unkindness, contempt, or pleasure in others' harm.

Scoring

Each measure is scored from 0 to 3

0At the baseline. Nothing to note.
1Mild strain. The person notices it; it costs others nothing.
2Clear strain. It regularly costs the person or the people around them.
3Severe. The person cannot function on this measure, or others are being harmed.

Who is the judge?

This is the hard part, and HMR does not pretend otherwise. A person who thinks they are admired may be admired by one friend and by no one else. HMR's working answer is that on the measures about other people, the reference is the honest response of many people, not the person's own estimate and not one observer's opinion: roughly, how people in general would respond if they saw the behavior.

On the measures about reality, the reference is what is the case. On the body, it is the body, measured. That is why the model needs four sources: conversation, the body, the people around the person, and their record of doing what they set out to do.

A name that says what is wrong

A score sheet replaces the label. Instead of one word, a description is built from the layers:

reality strain (which measures, how far)
+ self strain (which measures, how far)
+ strain with others (which measures, how far)

“Generalized anxiety” becomes “danger read too high (A2, 2) with a braced body (B2, 2)”. Two people with the same label today can have different descriptions, and two people with different labels can turn out to have the same one. That is what HMR expects the data to show.

Where to start

A decision tree, deepest layer first

Because the layers rest on one another, the order of questions matters. Work on the deepest strained layer first: correcting how someone treats people does little if their picture of themselves is far off, and correcting that does little if their picture of reality is.

  1. Is the body in distress? Pain, no sleep, chronic bracing. If so, start there, whatever else is found. A strained body colors every other measure.
  2. Is the picture of reality far off? The world, danger, time, what can be changed. If so, the work is a truer picture, and the other layers are read again afterward.
  3. Is the picture of self far off? Far too large, far too small, or far from the body. If so, the work is honest feedback from many people, given kindly.
  4. Is the strain only with other people? Boundaries, listening, empathy, adapting, kindness. These can be taught and practiced directly.
  5. Score again later. The description is a map of now. It is meant to change.
Every person has a profile

Put the scores together and each person has a shape. It can be drawn, tracked over time, and compared with the body's own profile from positional imaging, so that changes in one can be matched to changes in the other.

HMR's prediction is that once minds are mapped this way, many conditions psychiatry treats as separate will turn out to be the same shapes under different names, and the map will show why they overlap.

Today's labels, on the map

Why the categories blur

Here is where some of today's diagnoses might sit. These are HMR's hypotheses about where to look, offered so they can be tested. They are not diagnoses. HMR's prediction is that many conditions treated as separate will turn out to be different combinations of strain on the same few measures.

Today's labelLayerWhere HMR would look first
DepressionReality + selfPerspective and purpose very low; a picture of reality in which nothing matters or nothing will change; a heavy, collapsed body. HMR adds a question today's manual does not ask: is the sadness an accurate response to what the person has lived through and sees in the world?
Generalized anxietyRealityDanger read too high everywhere (A2); often a sense of too little time (A4); a braced body and shallow breath (B2).
Depression and anxiety togetherReality + selfThe same strain on the picture of reality and on the body, read from two sides. One reason the two are so often diagnosed together.
Bipolar disorderSelfVery deep and fast-changing feeling (B3) with swings in agency (B4). HMR's hypothesis is that depth of feeling is not itself the illness: depth with quick recovery would count as health, and what harms is being unable to come back.
PsychosisRealityA picture of reality far from what is the case (A1), with the other dimensions following from it.
SchizophreniaReality + self + othersDelusions and hallucinations, a picture of reality far from what is the case (A1); a marked drop in self-care and loss of drive and expression (B3, B4); a marked drop in work and in relations with others (C). These are among the criteria in the manual (American Psychiatric Association, DSM-5, 2013).
Major neurocognitive disorderReality + self + othersA significant decline in memory, attention, language, perception, or social cognition that interferes with independence in everyday activities (American Psychiatric Association, DSM-5, 2013). The decline reaches what the person can know of the world (A), of themselves (B), and how they live among others (C).

The middle of the map

Most labels sit in one layer or two. The middle is where a condition's defining criteria touch reality, self, and others at once. Schizophrenia and major neurocognitive disorder are placed there because the manual's own criteria for each reach all three layers. HMR reads this as the most pervasive kind of strain, since the layers rest on one another and strain at the base is carried up through all of them.

Where HMR differs most

Four readings that depart from today's model

Each is a hypothesis about what to measure. None is a reason to stop a treatment.

Some sadness is accurate

By HMR's own account, people are not treating each other, or other creatures, well. A person who sees that clearly, year after year, may be sad for good reason. Calling that a disorder locates the fault in the person who noticed. HMR would ask first whether the sadness fits the facts, and treat the cause where it does. Depression that does not fit the facts, or that stops a person from living, is a different thing and needs care.

Deep feeling is not the illness

Society teaches people not to cry in public. HMR reads that as a society uncomfortable with feeling it has not learned to manage. To feel something fully, to cry, and to be steady again in minutes is a sign of health. The aim is not to feel less. It is to recover well. On this reading, the capacity for very deep and fast feeling that today is labeled bipolar could, with quick recovery, be a strength.

A self-picture far off is serious

Someone who is sure people admire them when they do not, who offers advice nobody asked for, and does not notice the response, is steering by a false map of themselves. Today's model treats this lightly unless it is extreme. HMR treats it as a central sign, because every other measure is steered by the picture of self. The opposite error counts too: a person with good ideas who is too meek to share them.

Kindness is a measure of sanity

In HMR every creature is the same intelligence in another body. A person in tune with that is kind without effort, and appreciates what is good. Habitual anger, contempt, and cruelty are then signs of a mind out of tune with what is real, whatever the person's success. By this measure some people who do very well in society are less sane than they look.

How it would be measured

Four sources

Conversation about the person's picture of reality and values; positional imaging and tension mapping of the body; observation of how they engage with others, including family; and their record of setting and reaching goals.

What it changes

Treatment aims at the measure

A wrong picture of reality is answered with a truer one; a strained body with the fascia protocol; missing values with values; isolation with people. Medication, where used, would be tracked against the whole profile, including the body.

In short

HMR describes a sound mind by fourteen measures in three layers: how a person meets reality, how they see themselves, and how they meet others. A troubled mind is described by where it has moved away from those measures, so every person has a profile and not only a label. The page places today's diagnostic labels on that map, from conditions that touch one layer to those, such as schizophrenia, that touch all three. HMR expects conditions now treated as separate to turn out to be the same shapes under different names.
