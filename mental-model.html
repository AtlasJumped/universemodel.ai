The HMR mental health model.

Psychiatry divides the mind's troubles by lists of symptoms, and the lists overlap, blur and are applied inconsistently (the evidence). The HMR proposes a different map. It starts from a baseline, that the HMR's account of reality is true, and asks how a person stands in relation to it.

The dimensions

Six questions, instead of a symptom list.

Instead of asking which symptoms a person reports, the HMR asks how a person relates to the world. In this model, symptoms are not isolated items; they are signs of where on these six dimensions a person is under strain.

1

Picture of reality

How far the person's model of reality deviates from what is true: about themselves, about the world, about other people, about what life is. Any deviation from the baseline produces symptoms sooner or later, because a wrong map leads to wrong expectations, and gaps in the map fill with fear.

2

Body and tension

The state of the body's tensegrity: chronic tension, bracing, restricted breath, pain, sleep. In the HMR, a strained body changes how a person thinks and feels, and the brain's map of the body can drift from the body itself. The two maps →

3

Meeting others

How the person engages with society. Do they respect the boundaries of communication, listen as well as speak, cooperate, notice other people's needs? Someone who seems wholly self-absorbed, or who repeatedly breaks the ordinary limits of conversation, is showing strain here.

4

Values and purpose

Whether the person has values at all, whether they have reasons behind them, and whether their actions match them. A life lived against one's own values is a source of distress in itself. HMR Values →

5

Goals and agency

Whether the person can set out to do something and reliably do it. If an intelligence has a desire and can achieve it, a rationale is at work.

6

Perspective

The skill of finding the good in a situation and of appreciation. In the HMR, perspective is the main factor in happiness, and it can be practiced.

A map of a mind

Every person has a profile.

Put the six dimensions together and each person has a shape: a profile of where they are strong and where they are strained. It can be drawn, tracked over time and compared with the body's own profile from positional imaging, so that changes in one can be matched to changes in the other.

The HMR's prediction is that once minds are mapped this way, many conditions psychiatry treats as separate will turn out to be the same profiles under different names, and the map will show why they overlap: they are not separate disorders but different combinations of strain on the same few dimensions.

Today's labels, on the map

Why the categories blur.

Here is how some common diagnoses might sit on the HMR's map. These are the HMR's hypotheses about where to look, offered so they can be tested; they are not diagnoses.

Today's labelWhere the HMR would look first
DepressionPerspective and purpose very low; a picture of reality in which nothing matters or nothing will change; a heavy, collapsed body.
AnxietyA picture of reality that predicts threat everywhere; a braced, tense body and shallow breath.
Depression and anxiety togetherThe same strain on reality and the body, read from two sides. One reason the two are so often diagnosed together.
Bipolar disorderSwings in goals and agency and in how certain the picture of reality feels; often first labeled depression, because only one side of the swing was seen.
PsychosisA picture of reality far from the baseline, with the other dimensions following from it.
Personality disordersLong-standing strain in meeting others: boundaries, empathy, cooperation.
Post-traumatic stressA body and a body map reshaped by an event, and a picture of reality rebuilt around danger.
How it would be measured

Four sources

Conversation about the person's picture of reality and values; positional imaging and tension mapping of the body; observation of how they engage with others, including family; and their record of setting and reaching goals.

What it changes

Treatment aims at the dimension

A wrong picture of reality is answered with a truer one; a strained body with the fascia protocol; missing values with values; isolation with people. Medication, where used, would be tracked against the whole profile, including the body.
