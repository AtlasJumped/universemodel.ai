Terms

This is a short list of terms we think are critical to this conversation. We are nuanced and picky about the words we use, and this is what we are going with for now.

There are still other words to pick, and this page is probably not very interesting to most people. But we are carefully curating the language with which to express these ideas.

Context first

Every term sits in a context

A word can mean one thing in one context and another thing in the next, so the context comes before the term. The first layer is Universal Intelligence, the point of perspective. The second layer splits into physical reality and non-physical reality. Each branch then holds a list of narrower contexts.

How we choose

How a word earns its place

  1. Context comes first. Every term is placed under Universal Intelligence, then under physical or non-physical reality, then under a narrower context.
  2. Truth. Is there any way, shape, or form in which the word is untrue? Any departure from the truth puts it lower.
  3. Fidelity. Among words that are all true, the more exact one wins. A human is a biotensegrity, not just a tensegrity.
  4. A thing plus a property. A plain noun with a plain describing word, such as connective tissue, carries its meaning with almost no loss. It is preferred to a Latin or Greek term most people do not know, which goes in parentheses.
  5. Fewer words. We count the terms used across the HMR family of websites and map each group of words that mean the same thing back to one preferred word for each context.

Know a word that should be on this list, or one that does not exist yet and should? Tell us.

In short

HMR uses a small set of words with care. Each term sits in a context under Universal Intelligence and either physical or non-physical reality. A word has to be true first, then as exact as possible, and a plain thing-plus-property word beats a Latin one. We prefer deviation to change, ideal body to perfect body, connective tissue (often called fascia) to fascia, and biotensegrity to tensegrity for a human.
