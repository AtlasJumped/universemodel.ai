Before the Big Bang · for skeptics

"You want me to believe in God. Where is the science?"

A fair question, answered in one page. Science explains very well what happened after the Big Bang. The Holistic Model of Reality (HMR) adds what science does not attempt: a step-by-step account of how a non-physical intelligence became a physical universe, with no step that breaks logic.

The rest of the site covers the detail: the gaps in physics, Universal Intelligence, health.

What science covers

After the Big Bang

Physics traces the universe from a tiny fraction of a second after its beginning to today, 13.8 billion years later, and its predictions are confirmed again and again.

Where science stops

The beginning itself

Before about 10-43 seconds, the Planck time, its two best theories cannot be used together, and general relativity heads toward infinities (where the math conflicts). Why there was a Big Bang at all, science does not say.

What the HMR adds

How the transition happened

Traditions say God created the world. The HMR goes further and defines the segue: what existed first, why it changed, and the first thoughts that became geometry, distance and the physical universe.

From non-physical to physical

The transition, step by step.

Each step follows from the one before it. Step through the figure, or tap any step in the list. Every step is the HMR's hypothesis; what matters here is whether the chain holds together.

Step 1. Unlimited intelligence, every thought at once.

  1. Phase 1

    A non-physical, unlimited intelligence

    Before physical reality there is only Universal Intelligence (UI): no space, no time, no matter, and no limits. An intelligence that can think every thought at once has no reason to think one thought rather than another. In human terms that state is close to what we would call insanity, and it would not have been a pleasant one.

  2. Phase 2

    A limit must be imposed, and it is self-imposed

    Something unlimited can change only by taking on a limit. If UI is all that exists, nothing outside it can impose one, so the limit is self-imposed. And a chosen limit is not random: UI limited itself to focus its thoughts in more pleasant ways. The pleasant thoughts were the orderly ones, ideas that lead to further ideas, as a framework does. Mathematical thoughts make patterns, patterns make prediction possible, and predictability is a form of stability.

  3. The first mechanized thought

    Me, and not me

    The first framework is identity. To know what I am, I must tell myself apart from what I am not. The smallest possible distinction needs exactly two things: a point that is me, and a point imagined as not me. Nothing simpler can carry a difference.

  4. Distance

    Two points imply a distance, and a line

    Two points that are different cannot sit in the same place, so something must separate them: an imagined distance. Distance is the first quantity, and it is a relationship rather than a thing. The shortest path from one point to the other is a line, the first dimension. To move from a reality with no space to a reality with space, distance is exactly what has to be mechanized.

  5. π

    A distance with no preferred direction gives a circle, and π

    Only me exists to give "not me" a position, and nothing marks one direction as special. So "not me, at that distance" is not one point but every point at that distance: a circle in a plane, a sphere in space. The ratio of any circle's distance around to its distance across is the same number, π. It is not chosen; it is forced, once distance exists with no favored direction. This is why the HMR places π among the first thoughts. More on π →

    Where an assumption enters: two points alone do not yield π. It appears when distance is the same in every direction and continuous. The HMR's argument is that a self-imposed limit, with nothing else in existence, has no source for a favored direction. Whether that step can be derived formally or must be assumed is open mathematical work.

  6. Relationship and dimension

    Each new independent relation adds a dimension

    A third point that is not on the line cannot be described by the first distance alone; it requires a second, independent direction, and the three points make a plane. A fourth point off that plane requires a third. Dimension is the number of independent relations needed to say where things are. Points, lines, planes, ratios and π: these are the original thought bases, the perspectives from which a non-spatial intelligence could think about what it is and what it is not.

  7. The Big Bang

    The thought becomes physical

    In the HMR, the Big Bang is the moment UI settled on me and not me, and with it on distance and π. From that structure came shapes, energies and the interactions between them: intelligence exploring what it is and what it is not, testing limits and boundaries. The stable rules of that exploration are what we measure as the laws of physics. From here, the account and mainstream science run together.

Honest about the claim

What this shows, and what it does not.

It does not
  • Prove that UI exists. No step above is a measurement.
  • Replace any established result of physics.
  • Yet give the full mathematics that takes distance and π all the way to spacetime. That mathematics still has to be written and checked.
  • Ask anyone to take it on faith. Each step is labelled a hypothesis, and the step from distance to π names the assumption it rests on.

What it offers instead is a chain to inspect. A skeptic does not have to accept the conclusion; they only have to find the link that breaks.

It does
  • Tell one narrative that never breaks logic, from a non-physical intelligence to the physical universe.
  • Include all of science within it, unchanged, from the Big Bang onward.
  • Add what science lacks: why there is a universe, why it runs on mathematics, and what came before.
  • Bridge a God that existed and the Big Bang, instead of leaving a gap between them.
  • Lead to practical work: a physics-based model of the healthy body that can be simulated and tested.

If no one can find a break in the framework, and it explains more than science while losing nothing science has established, then it carries more information than the scientific picture alone.

That is the HMR's standard for itself: not "believe this", but "find the step that fails".
