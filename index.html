Universe Model

Humanity has no holistic model of reality.

Physics describes matter, medicine describes illness, and the great religions describe meaning, but none of them describes the whole. A holistic model of reality would be one account of all of it: how physical reality arises, what a human being is, and how a human body works and fails.

The Holistic Model of Reality (HMR) proposes that physical reality emerges from an underlying non-physical intelligence process, driven by Universal Intelligence (UI). If that is right, it matters to everyone. It changes what we understand ourselves to be, describes a reality that responds to us, and sets very different limits on a human life, and a human body, than a purely material view allows.

This site is not here to persuade you of that view. We start with its most tangible part: a new model of health, built on physics and data. As AI advances, the aim is to model the whole chain, from the intelligence process beneath physical reality to the human body, and with it to simulate and reverse-engineer disease, opening new paths to treatment.

The problem

We use unhealthy humans as the reference point.

Almost everything medicine knows about the human body was learned by studying people who were already unwell, and by averaging across populations in which few people are fully healthy. On top of that sits an assumption: that aging is a normal, steady slide into stiffness, pain and disease, and that most of it is simply our destiny.

In a body understood as a tensegrity, that assumption is wrong. So every day, people measure themselves, their families and the people they love against the wrong bar.

It does not matter how many people we study. Thousands or millions, the reference point is still the wrong one, and the whole of medical history so far has been built on it.

The right reference point is a healthy human body, defined by physics. That is what the HMR provides: a measured model of the ideal body, against which every symptom and disease becomes a deviation that can be traced and, we hope, reversed.

The reference point today

  • Built from studies and trials of people who are already sick.
  • "Normal" ranges drawn from populations that include many people who are not well.
  • Decline with age treated as expected.
  • Care begins at symptoms and often ends at managing them.

The reference point we are building

  • An ideal human body, male and female, from real measurements with real physical limits.
  • Physics as the framework: tension, pressure, stretch and shape.
  • Every condition measured as a deviation from the ideal.
  • The path from ideal to disease traced, to find root causes and ways back.
Why the body first

The body as a tensegrity.

Our working hypothesis is that the human body is best understood as a tensegrity: bones held in place by a continuous web of tensioned connective tissue called fascia, which wraps every muscle, organ, vessel and nerve without a break.

If that is right, then the shape of a body, how it moves, and where it stiffens, sticks or stores fat are not random. They follow from physics. And physics can be modeled, measured and compared.

Our central hypothesis: make the fascia denser in one place, leave the rest ideal, and the physics alone will reshape the body elsewhere and strain the vessels, nerves and organs that run through it, in patterns that match real diseases.
