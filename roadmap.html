Road Ahead

From a library to a new paradigm for health

The first stretch of this road is already behind us: having the idea, working it through, and setting it down in full for the first time. This website is that work. After years of thought, hard criticism, and testing on our own bodies through the connective tissue protocol, the model has held up, and it rings true.

Now two things happen at once. We are sharing the model, here, and we are collecting the evidence to test it. An idea this large will only be adopted when people can see for themselves that it is good, so the work ahead is to make that plain: build a model of the ideal human body, and put it to work. In HMR's own terms, an idea like this is Universal Intelligence coming to know itself through people. Our responsibility is to pass it on properly.

Where the road leads, if HMR is right, is what the traditions called a golden age: a humanity that is healthy, agrees on what reality is, and cooperates. We mean that as a logical consequence of the model, not as a slogan. The future HMR describes →

  1. Largely done

    Collect

    A team of AI research agents gathered the research, anatomy, measurements, and images that the twelve datasets need, free and open sources first. Gaps are still being filled.

  2. Largely done

    Index and organize

    Local AI models on our own hardware read, sort, and connect everything, so any question can be answered from the evidence in seconds, including the evidence against us.

  3. Now

    Model the ideal human body

    Build the male and female reference bodies layer by layer, bones to experience, with real measurements and real limits, and check them against what real bodies can and cannot do. Then run the first test: the densification hypothesis.

  4. Then

    Run the simulation campaign

    Deploy millions of simulated bodies carrying millions to billions of different combinations of imperfections, and let the physics show which combinations produce which conditions. The plan below →

  5. Then

    Trace disease and test

    Compare what the simulations produce with images and measurements of people living with specific conditions, turn the matches into predictions others can test, and publish what holds up and what does not.

  6. Later

    Share and build

    Explain the work openly, to researchers and to the public, and build the partnerships, materials, products, and centers needed to take it further.

The first human trial

What it would take to bring this to people

The simulation tests the model in physics. The connective tissue protocol also has to be tested in people. We have designed the first human trial from start to finish.

Who

1,000 veterans, for one year

United States veterans, whose bodies carry imperfections from service and who know how to live by a mission, on a campus beside a military base in Southern California.

What

The protocol, every day

Three short showers, at least 30 minutes of immersion, stretching, and movement, inside a community built on HMR's values. Knee function and chronic pain are measured on published scales.

Then

Graduates carry it outward

At six months, leaders are chosen. After the year, teams open new sites in United States cities and then abroad. A second cohort adds families and day members.

It is a design, not a running study. Nothing has been funded or approved, and it needs a principal investigator. The full page covers the hypotheses, the facility, a day in the life, the budget, and the ethics.

The simulation campaign

Not one body tuned by hand. Millions, tested by physics

Clicking around a single model until it looks like a disease would prove nothing. The plan is to build one ideal body, define every way it can be made imperfect, and then run the combinations at a scale no person could search by hand.

Even a modest version, a few hundred places on the body, a few settings for each tissue property, and three imperfections per body, gives more combinations than anyone could ever try one at a time, so the campaign samples them systematically. If an ideal biotensegrity and a complete set of imperfections reproduce the shapes and measurements of real conditions, without anyone telling the model what those conditions look like, that is evidence the model is describing reality. If they do not, we learn exactly where it is wrong.

The ideal biotensegrity, set up once

A male and a female body at time zero: every bone, every connective tissue layer, and every embedded structure, each tissue given its measured stiffness, stretch, shear, and compression limits from the literature. Healthy tissue is shown in blue. Nothing in this body is tuned to produce a disease.

An architecture of every way to be imperfect

Each imperfection is a defined object placed at a location and a time, with a boundary: inside it are its local qualities, outside it are its downstream effects. Imperfections are shown in red. The properties an imperfection can carry, a list that will keep growing as the model is built:

PointsA single site of change, with a boundary around it.
LinesA band of change along a tension line or seam.
PlanesA sheet of change across a connective tissue layer.
RegionsA three-dimensional pocket of change inside or between tissues.
TorqueA joint or limb twisted the wrong way by changed tension.
DensityHow much denser the tissue is than ideal.
FrictionHow much less freely layers slide over each other.
AdhesionLayers stuck together that should glide.
MassRelative mass added or lost at the site.
VolumeRelative swelling or shrinking at the site.
PressureFluid and compartment pressure inside and around the site.
Resting tensionHow much pre-stress the tissue carries at rest.
StiffnessHow hard the tissue resists stretch (its elastic modulus).
Tensile strengthHow much pull it takes to begin to fail.
Compression strengthHow much squeeze it can bear.
Tear strengthHow easily a cut or weak point spreads.
Shear strengthHow it resists layers being pushed past each other.
Creep and relaxationHow tissue slowly lengthens under steady load, and how its tension fades.
HydrationWater content, which changes sliding and stiffness.
ThicknessLayers thickened or thinned from the ideal.
Fiber directionWhich way collagen fibers run, and how orderly they are.
Cross-linkingHow tightly fibers are bound to each other.
TemperatureLocal warmth or cold, which changes tissue behavior.
LengthTissue shortened or lengthened between its attachments.
SymmetryLeft and right, front and back, matched or not.
BoundariesThe territory of local effect, and its creep along vessels and nerves.
TimeWhen it begins, how fast it grows, and whether it fades or heals.

Deploy at scale

Millions of simulated tensegrities, each carrying a different sampled combination of imperfections, run forward in time on the GPUs. Every run is recorded: where tension redistributed, which vessels and nerves were compressed, how posture and symmetry changed.

Prove it models reality, without architecting the answers

The hardest part is honesty. A model with enough knobs can be made to produce anything, so the campaign is built so that it cannot be steered toward the result we hope for:

Blinded designImperfections are sampled without reference to any disease label. The model never sees what a condition is supposed to look like.
Compared afterwardsOutcomes are matched, only after the runs, against measured disease shapes from real data: imaging, body shape, and clinical numbers.
Pre-registered predictionsWhat we expect to see is written down and dated before the comparison is made.
Held-out dataPart of the real data is set aside and never used while the model is built, then used once to test it.
Negative controlsRandom, shuffled, and physically meaningless imperfections are run too. If they match disease as well as the real ones, the result means nothing.
Failures reportedConditions the model cannot reproduce are published alongside the ones it can.

Turn results into something useful

A map of each conditionIf we can start from the ideal biotensegrity, add specific imperfections and arrive at a measured condition, we have a step-by-step account of how that condition may develop, from the first change to the last.
A way backA path that can be run forward can be studied in reverse. For each mapped condition, the model can propose which imperfections, released in which order, would lead back toward the ideal, as research hypotheses to be tested properly.
What else is going onThe same runs show every other change the imperfections cause: distant tension, compressed vessels, altered posture. Some may be things in the body medicine is not yet accounting for, and they become new questions for researchers and clinicians.

No one has ever modeled the human body this deeply. The ideal body is built once; then it becomes an instrument for running the experiments medicine has never been able to run.

How the model will be used

As a reference. A measured picture of the healthy body against which any body can be compared.

As a map of causes. For a given condition, a traced path from the ideal to the body that has it, pointing to where things first went wrong.

As a test bed. A way to ask, before anyone tries anything on a person, what a change to the body's mechanics would be expected to do.

As a teacher. A body that can be watched moving through every yoga pose and extreme movement, showing what the human design allows.

In the future, an avatar for every person. If this becomes a humanity-wide project with the resources to match, every person could have a physics-based avatar of their own body, built from regular imaging as a standard part of healthcare. You could ask it what an exercise would do, which exercises would best optimize your biotensegrity, or what a change in diet would do, down to the effect of a single molecule. That is the far goal. In the meantime, we are reverse-engineering disease.

How we will know if we are wrong

A model of the ideal body makes predictions. If it produces something real bodies show to be impossible, or fails to explain the shapes that conditions actually produce, it is wrong in that place, and we will say so.

That is why the evidence against the model is collected from the very start, and why the campaign runs negative controls and reports its failures.

The long-term vision

A connective tissue care protocol for every person on Earth

The long-term vision is simple to state: every human being has a connective tissue care protocol as a basic part of their daily health routine, as ordinary as brushing their teeth. Ask anyone what their connective tissue protocol is, and they can tell you.

If the model holds up, it gives that protocol a foundation health care has never had: a clear picture of what full health is, and a way to measure the distance to it. This site exists to help create the materials the full protocol needs, and to keep them grounded in physics and evidence.

As common as brushing your teeth.

Building the protocol has already meant testing well over a hundred tools and moisturizers, day after day. That hands-on experience shows exactly where today's tools fall short, and what the protocol needs instead.

Education

Open materials that teach how the body works as a biotensegrity and how to care for its connective tissue, for adults and especially for children as they grow.

New products

Tools that do not exist yet, designed for the protocol from scratch, such as toe spacers and toe-spacing socks made to work as one system.

Connective tissue health centers

Places dedicated to connective tissue care. What they look like and the equipment they include have already been designed.

Better products

Improvements on tools that already exist, often slight, worked out through years of using them: scrapers, massage guns, moisturizers, and more.

A golden age of health, foretold

Ancient traditions describe a golden age when people lived long and well. Hindu texts describe the Satya Yuga, an age of truth when people lived far longer and suffered little. The Greek poet Hesiod described a golden race who lived without toil or the miseries of old age. The prophet Isaiah pictured a world where no infant would die young. HMR meets the conditions of that picture: a body restored to its ideal, a clear account of what reality is, and values shared by everyone.

All it asks of anyone is to take it seriously, and share it.
