The people and work that shaped HMR

HMR did not come from nowhere. These are the teachers, thinkers, scientists, and channels we learned the most from on the way here, with links to the best places to find their work.

None of them is affiliated with HMR, and none has endorsed it. Where we say what HMR shares with someone, that is our reading of their work.

Teachers and thinkers

Four people whose work aligns with HMR

S

Sadhguru

Born 3 September 1957 in Mysore, India. Living.

Life and work

Sadhguru is a yogi and teacher. He was born Jagadish Vasudev in Mysore, India, on 3 September 1957. He earned a degree in English literature at the University of Mysore and declined postgraduate study. He built a poultry farm, then co-founded a construction firm, Buildaids. On 23 September 1982, on Chamundi Hill in Mysore, he had his first spiritual experience. A similar one followed about six days later, and about six weeks after that he closed his businesses.

He gave his first yoga class in Mysore in 1983 and founded the Isha Foundation in 1992. In 1994 he bought land near the Velliangiri mountains in Coimbatore and opened the Isha Yoga Center there, where in 2017 he consecrated the 112-foot Adiyogi statue. He has written more than thirty books, including the New York Times bestseller Inner Engineering, and in 2017 he was awarded the Padma Vibhushan, one of India's highest civilian honors.

What he is doing now

Save Soil continues as an international campaign. It began as a 100-day motorcycle journey of about 30,000 km from London to southern India in 2022, and it followed Rally for Rivers (2017) and Cauvery Calling (2019). In 2026 he led a Mahasatsang at Batu Caves in Malaysia, attended by more than 8,000 people and described as the first held outside India. In India, the foundation's outreach arm now operates crematoria for the state of Bihar.

He and the foundation have also drawn criticism, including for some health claims, and the foundation is party to legal disputes, which it contests.

What HMR shares with him

We took the Inner Engineering course. In HMR's view his techniques are very good for fascia, and his values, though he does not know of HMR, are naturally in tune with it.

AW

Alan Watts

Born 6 January 1915 in Chislehurst, England. Died 16 November 1973 in Marin County, California.

Life and work

Alan Watts was a British-born writer and speaker who interpreted Buddhist, Taoist, and Hindu thought for Western audiences. He called himself a "philosophical entertainer". He was born in Chislehurst, Kent, on 6 January 1915, and at 16 he was secretary of the London Buddhist Lodge. In 1936 he met D. T. Suzuki and published The Spirit of Zen. He moved to the United States in 1938, served as an Episcopal priest, and left the priesthood in 1950.

From 1951 to 1957 he taught at the American Academy of Asian Studies in San Francisco, and from 1953 to 1962 he built a following with a weekly radio program on KPFA in Berkeley. His books include The Wisdom of Insecurity (1951), The Way of Zen (1957), Psychotherapy East and West (1961), and The Book: On the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are (1966). He died on 16 November 1973 at Druid Heights, his cabin on Mount Tamalpais, aged 58.

His central idea

The feeling of being a separate self, an "ego in a bag of skin", is a mistake. Each of us is an aspect of one whole. He spent his life showing Western listeners how Eastern thought arrives there.

What HMR shares with him

He married East and West, which is what HMR sets out to do. His gift was saying it clearly. Listening to him opened us to ideas from the East that we had never come across growing up in the West, and it can reshape how a person thinks.

Many hours of his talks are on YouTube. We like to listen while falling asleep.

TM

Terence McKenna

Born 16 November 1946 in Paonia, Colorado. Died 3 April 2000 in San Rafael, California.

Life and work

Terence McKenna was an American ethnobotanist, author, and lecturer. In 1969 he went to Nepal to study Tibetan Bon shamanism. He worked as a hashish smuggler until US Customs seized a shipment, and later he collected butterflies in Indonesia. After his mother's death in 1970 he and his brother Dennis traveled to the Colombian Amazon, looking for a DMT preparation called oo-koo-hé. They found Psilocybe cubensis mushrooms instead. At La Chorrera they experimented with mushrooms, harmine, and vocal techniques, and Terence described contact with a voice he called the "Logos". The journey is told in True Hallucinations (1993).

The brothers wrote The Invisible Landscape in 1975, and in 1976 they published a mushroom growers' guide under the pseudonyms O. T. Oss and O. N. Oeric. In 1985 he and his wife, Kathleen Harrison, founded Botanical Dimensions, a nonprofit ethnobotanical preserve in Hawaii. Some of his best-known proposals, such as the "stoned ape" idea and novelty theory, are not accepted by science. He died of brain cancer on 3 April 2000, aged 53.

Dennis McKenna

Through Terence we came to his brother Dennis (born 1950), an ethnopharmacologist with a doctorate from the University of British Columbia, a founding board member of the Heffter Research Institute, and the founder of the McKenna Academy of Natural Philosophy (2019). He has spent a career studying these plants as a scientist.

What HMR shares with him

He was unafraid to explore the limits of the human psyche, and he trusted his own experience of reality over what society told him to think. He did it his way and shared what he found. In HMR's terms that is living true to your own model of reality.

These substances are illegal in most places. HMR's view is that they deserve careful, responsible, legal research, and the McKenna brothers' work is part of why that research now exists.

BS

Baruch Spinoza

Born 24 November 1632 in Amsterdam. Died 21 February 1677 in The Hague.

Life and work

Baruch Spinoza was a Dutch philosopher, born in Amsterdam on 24 November 1632 to a Portuguese-Jewish merchant family that had fled Portugal. He had a traditional Jewish schooling, then studied Latin with Franciscus van den Enden, who introduced him to Descartes. On 27 July 1656 the Amsterdam Portuguese-Jewish community expelled him for his views, at the age of 23. He supported himself by grinding lenses.

In 1663 he published his exposition of Descartes' Principles of Philosophy, the only book that appeared under his name in his lifetime. His Theological-Political Treatise (1670) was published anonymously, banned, and caused an uproar across Europe. He turned down a university chair at Heidelberg to keep his freedom of thought. His chief work, the Ethics, is laid out like Euclid's geometry, with definitions, axioms, and proofs, which makes him one of the first to reason about God with the rigor of geometry. He died in The Hague on 21 February 1677, aged 44, probably of tuberculosis. The Ethics was published that year.

His central idea

There is one infinite substance, which he called God or Nature (Deus sive Natura). Everything that exists is a way that one substance is. Mind and body are the same thing understood in two ways.

What HMR shares with him

One reality, with mind and body as two sides of it, reasoned step by step: that is the shape of HMR. He also paid the price of holding a model of reality that his community would not accept, and held it anyway.

Scientists who share it

Three physicists who make science exciting for everyone

Each of them does serious physics, and each has put a great deal of exciting, entertaining work in public, where people who are not scientists can enjoy it.

Born 24 January 1947, San Jose, California

Michio Kaku

Professor of theoretical physics at the City College of New York and a co-founder of string field theory: in 1974 he and Keiji Kikkawa wrote the first papers putting string theory in field form.

His books for general readers include Hyperspace (1994), Physics of the Impossible (2008), Physics of the Future (2011), The Future of the Mind (2014), and The God Equation (2021). He hosts the radio program Science Fantastic and has presented many television series on the future of science.

mkaku.orgYouTube
Born 5 October 1958, New York City

Neil deGrasse Tyson

An astrophysicist with a doctorate from Columbia University, and director of the Hayden Planetarium at the American Museum of Natural History since 1996.

He hosted Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey (2014) and has hosted the StarTalk podcast since 2009. His books include Death by Black Hole (2007) and Astrophysics for People in a Hurry (2017).

neildegrassetyson.comYouTube
Born 9 February 1963, New York City

Brian Greene

Professor of physics and mathematics at Columbia University, where he directs the center for theoretical physics. A string theorist, he co-discovered mirror symmetry.

His books include The Elegant Universe (1999), The Fabric of the Cosmos (2004), The Hidden Reality (2011), and Until the End of Time (2020). He hosted the NOVA series based on the first, and in 2008 he co-founded the World Science Festival with Tracy Day.

Channels we watch

Four channels that taught us a great deal

Science

Veritasium

Made by Derek Muller, who holds a doctorate in physics education research from the University of Sydney. Since 2011 the channel has taken on the ideas in science that people most often get wrong, with experiments and interviews. It is a model of how to teach in a way that is fun, creative, and exciting. We have learned a great deal from it.

veritasium.com
Physics and astronomy

PBS Space Time

Hosted and written since 2015 by Matt O'Dowd, an astrophysicist at Lehman College of the City University of New York. It is rigorous about the mathematics and still watchable for anyone curious. Its community on Discord gave HMR some strong criticism early on, and that criticism helped us get here.

PBS Space Time
Mathematics

3Blue1Brown

Made by Grant Sanderson, a Stanford mathematics graduate, since 2015. He teaches higher mathematics through animation, so that you watch an idea being invented instead of being handed a formula. To us it is the best mathematics channel there is.

3blue1brown.com
Health

Huberman Lab

Hosted by Andrew Huberman, associate professor of neurobiology and ophthalmology at Stanford. Since 2021 he has put long conversations about health science in front of the public, where they can be examined. Our views differ from those of many of his guests, and some scientists have criticized claims made on the show. The body of material it has opened up is very large and very useful.

hubermanlab.com

A few channels taught us physics, mathematics, and health along the way. None of them is affiliated with HMR or has endorsed it.
